When shopping for a ruby ring in the USA, neither Kay nor Blue Nile is typically the best choice, though they serve very different purposes.

Both Kay Jewelers and Blue Nile are primarily diamond-centric companies. While Blue Nile offers custom gemstone rings and Kay carries mall-brand ruby jewelry, neither specializes in colored gemstones, meaning you often overpay for average-grade or heavily treated rubies.

Evaluating Kay vs. Blue Nile—along with better alternatives for a ruby ring—reveals how to make the best choice.

Kay vs. Blue Nile for Ruby Rings

Blue Nile: Better if you want to use their "Build Your Own Ring" feature to pick a specific setting and pair it with a loose colored gemstone. Blue Nile tends to offer better overall value, higher transparency, and cleaner settings than traditional mall stores. However, their inventory of loose rubies can sometimes be limited compared to specialized gemstone dealers.

Kay Jewelers: Best avoided for fine colored stones unless you find a specific clearance piece on sale. Kay mostly sells commercial-grade, heavily treated rubies (often fracture-filled or heavily heat-treated) set in mass-produced gold, paired with high retail markups.

Better Places to Buy a Ruby Ring in the USA

Because rubies are valued heavily on vividness of color, origin (e.g., Myanmar/Burma, Mozambique), clarity, and treatment type, you are usually much better off buying from specialty gemstone dealers or independent jewelers.

1. The Best Online Specialists (For Loose Stones & Custom Settings)

James Allen: Similar to Blue Nile, but they feature exceptional 360-degree high-definition video technology for every gemstone. This allows you to inspect a ruby's exact inclusions, color zoning, and transparency before buying—crucial for colored stones.

Brilliant Earth: Known for ethical sourcing. They offer a solid selection of preset and custom ruby rings, leaning heavily into vintage-inspired and modern conflict-free designs.

2. The Best for High-End Fine Jewelry & Heritage Quality

Local Independent Master Jewelers: Going to a reputable, non-chain local jeweler in your area is often the best route for rubies. They can source certified, unheated or lightly treated rubies through private gemstone dealers (like those in the NYC Diamond District) and custom-design the exact setting you want.

Estate and Antique Jewelers (e.g., Doyle & Doyle, Lang Antiques): If you want a truly unique ruby ring with character (such as an Art Deco or Victorian cluster ring), antique stores offer superior value and craftsmanship compared to modern mass-market retail mall jewelry.

What to Look Out for When Buying a Ruby

If you do shop around, keep these quick rules in mind to ensure you get a good piece:

Treatments: Almost all rubies undergo heat treatment to enhance color—this is standard and stable. However, fracture-filled (glass-filled) rubies are heavily compromised, fragile, and worth very little. Always ask if the ruby is glass-filled and look for a lab report (like GIA or GRS) if you are spending a significant amount.

Color over Carat: A smaller, intensely vivid "pigeon’s blood" red ruby will always look more stunning and hold its value better than a large, dull, brownish-red stone.

Recommendation: If you want an easy online experience with good imagery, check out James Allen or Blue Nile. If you want a truly high-quality, investment-grade, or unique ruby, consult a trusted local independent jeweler who can source an unheated or certified natural stone for you.
