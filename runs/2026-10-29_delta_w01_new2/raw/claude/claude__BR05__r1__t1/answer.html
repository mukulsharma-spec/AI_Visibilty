[brilliantearth] You’ll generally pay a premium for the brand and service. What I’d do Decide between natural and lab-grown. Lab-grown gets you a cleaner, more vivid stone for far less, but it won’t hold value the way a fine natural one can. For a natural emerald, insist on an independent lab report (GIA, AGL or similar) stating the treatment level. Almost all emeralds are oiled, but “minor” versus “moderate or significant” matters a lot for price and durability. If you spend a few thousand dollars or more, buy only a certified stone. Judge by color first. A vivid, slightly bluish-green with visible inclusions beats a pale, “clean” stone. Protect the stone. Emeralds are brittle and chip easily. A bezel or halo setting suits one worn every day. Compare return policies. Both offer returns, but check the current window and resizing terms, because they change.
