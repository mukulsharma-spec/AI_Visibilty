For a sapphire ring under $1,000, I’d pick Angara for most people. Search results were thin on current prices, so treat the specifics below as general guidance and check live listings.

Angara is a colored-gemstone specialist, and reviewers point to their gemstone expertise and extensive colored gemstone collection. That matters at your budget, because sapphire quality varies enormously. Angara grades its stones in tiers (Good, Better, Best, and so on) and offers some GIA-certified sapphires, so you can see what you’re paying for. Many of its sapphire rings fall under $1,000, usually with smaller stones (roughly 0.5 to 1 carat), or with lab-grown sapphires if you want more size for the money. Its sapphire pieces typically run from $500 to $2,000+, so filter carefully. Angara also runs frequent sales, so don’t pay full list price. [comparemaniac] [ajluxe]

Blue Nile is best known for diamonds, and sapphire is a smaller part of its catalog. The sapphire rings that turned up in my search were mostly diamond-halo styles priced well above your budget, such as an oval sapphire halo ring at $2,238 even after a 40% discount. It could work if you find a simple solitaire or a small sapphire in a style you love. Otherwise, you’ll likely get less sapphire per dollar. [goodmorningamerica]

Kay is the budget and convenience option. Its prices are low, but many of its sapphires are lab-created or treated, and the quality and disclosure are less transparent than at Angara. It’s a good choice if you want to see and try on the ring in a store, or want financing. If you go this route, ask for the stone’s origin (natural or lab-created) and any treatments, and compare against the “original” prices, since Kay is almost always on sale.

If you want a natural sapphire with clear grading, buy from Angara. If you want to shop in person, go to Kay, and make sure you ask about treatments. Only consider Blue Nile if you spot a specific design you like within your budget.
