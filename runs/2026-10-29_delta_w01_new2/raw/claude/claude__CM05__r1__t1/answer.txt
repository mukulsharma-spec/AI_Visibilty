Where to look depends on what you mean by “cheaper”: a cheaper natural sapphire, or a lab-grown or silver version that looks the same for much less.

Budget (under about $500)

- Ross-Simons sells sapphire tennis bracelets in sterling silver. Their listings show a 7.90 ct. t.w. sapphire tennis bracelet in sterling silver, rated 4.71 out of 5, from $435. They run frequent sales, so it’s worth waiting for one.
- Angara offers lab-grown blue sapphires, which are chemically, optically and physically the same as mined stones and cost much less. They also customize styles and metals.
- Walmart and Amazon carry created-sapphire sterling silver bracelets for far less. One reviewer on a created white sapphire silver bracelet called it an affordable alternative to a real diamond bracelet. Quality varies, so read the reviews and check the return policy.

Mid-range (about $500 to $1,500, real gold)

- eBay has plenty of natural sapphire tennis bracelets from independent jewelers. Some of its listings sit around $480 to $580, marked down from $659 to $799, and many accept offers. Only buy from sellers with high feedback and a return window. Ask for a gemstone description (natural vs. lab, treatment) in writing.
- Chairish and estate jewelers are good for vintage or pre-owned pieces with more carat weight for the money.
- Rare Carat is useful for comparing lab and natural stones. It lists a Lunava sapphire and lab diamond tennis bracelet, and it has Black Friday deals coming up.

Where Blue Nile sits: it usually costs more because of its brand, certificates and fine-jewelry standards. If you want to compare, ask Blue Nile for a price, then match it against Ross-Simons, Angara and a well-rated eBay jeweler.

How to save money

- Lab-grown or sterling silver: the biggest saver, and lab-grown sapphires look identical to natural ones.
- Sapphire-only or smaller stones: adding diamonds raises the price a lot.
- Bezel or secure settings: these matter more than carat weight for something you’ll wear daily.
- Buying in November: Black Friday tends to cut jewelry prices significantly.
- Natural sapphires: nearly all are heat-treated, which is normal. Make sure the seller discloses it.
