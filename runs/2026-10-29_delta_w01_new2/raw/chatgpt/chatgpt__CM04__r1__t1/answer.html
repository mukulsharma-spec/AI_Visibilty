What matters most when buying a ruby

- Natural vs. lab-created: Both are real ruby, but natural rubies are mined and generally cost more. Lab-created rubies can offer excellent color for less.
- Treatment disclosure: Many natural rubies are heat-treated. Ask whether the stone is heated, fracture-filled, or otherwise treated; treatments can significantly affect value.
- Color: Look for a vivid, rich red that still looks lively in ordinary indoor lighting, rather than one that appears very dark or brownish.
- Setting: For everyday wear, 14k or 18k gold with a secure setting is a sensible starting point.
- Returns: Read the actual policy before ordering. For online purchases, keep the ring unworn until you've decided to keep it.

One important distinction: a diamond grading report isn't the same as a ruby report. For a valuable natural ruby, ask for a reputable gemological laboratory report that identifies the stone and documents detectable treatments.
