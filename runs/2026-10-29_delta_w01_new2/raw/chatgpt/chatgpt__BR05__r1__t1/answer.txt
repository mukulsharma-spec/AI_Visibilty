How to avoid overpaying for an emerald

1. Prioritize the green color
Look for a rich, attractive green that appears reasonably even across the stone. A smaller emerald with excellent color can look more luxurious than a larger, pale or overly dark one.

2. Ask about treatments and clarity
Natural emeralds commonly undergo oil or resin treatments to improve apparent clarity. Ask which treatment was used, how significant it is, and whether the seller discloses it in writing. Inclusions are normal; avoid stones with fractures that look especially vulnerable to damage.

3. Choose a protective setting
Emerald is less durable in everyday wear than diamond or sapphire. A protective bezel or well-designed prong setting can help, and you'll want to remove the ring during workouts, housework and other activities that risk impact.

One important distinction: if you're considering a lab-grown emerald, don't compare its price or grading directly with a natural emerald as though they were equivalent purchases. Both can be beautiful, but natural origin generally commands a premium.
