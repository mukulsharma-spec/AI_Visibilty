Which labs to look for

For coloured stones, GIA, SSEF and Gübelin are the benchmark labs. One source notes that for high-value stones destined for auction or international dealers, reports from GIA, SSEF or Gübelin carry the broadest consensus on origin opinions. The UK also has its own lab, AnchorCert in Birmingham’s Assay Office, which serves the UK trade and consumers as a domestic alternative to the major international labs.

Before you buy

Ask for the lab report number and check it on the issuing lab’s website. A “certificate of authenticity” with no reference number and no way to verify it online is functionally worthless.


Be wary of in-house paperwork. A seller’s own certificate isn’t independent verification.
Check that the report matches the stone. Measurements, weight and treatment status should line up with what you’re shown.
Read the treatment line closely. Heat treatment is very common in sapphire and ruby, and “unheated” typically carries a large premium.
Check the return policy. Ask whether you can return the stone if an independent lab later disagrees with the seller’s description.

If you tell me the stone type and rough budget, I can narrow this down. For example, a sapphire under £2,000 and a £10,000+ ruby with an origin report would point you to different sellers.
