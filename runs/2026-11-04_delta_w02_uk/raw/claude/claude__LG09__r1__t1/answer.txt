Quality

A lab-created emerald is real emerald: the same mineral (beryl coloured by chromium/vanadium) with the same chemistry and crystal structure as a mined stone, just grown in a controlled setting. Compared with natural stones, the main differences are:

Clarity: Natural emeralds almost always have inclusions, often called “jardin” (French for garden), and these give each stone its character. Most natural stones have these inclusions. Lab stones are usually much cleaner and more consistent in colour. Some people love that, and others find them a bit too perfect. 
angara

Price: Lab stones are much cheaper. One retailer says lab-grown is priced up to 80% lower than natural emeralds, though that’s a seller’s figure. They also tend to come in larger sizes for the money. 
angara

Durability: Emeralds are brittle even though they’re fairly hard. Lab stones are less often fracture-filled or oiled than natural ones, but they still chip if knocked, so they suit a ring that won’t get daily knocks.
Resale value: Expect very little. Buy because you like it, not as an investment.

What to watch for

Cheap “lab emeralds” are sometimes green glass, synthetic spinel or another simulant. Ask for the stone to be described as lab-grown emerald (beryl) and for the growth method (flux or hydrothermal).
Check that the seller will say plainly whether the stone has any treatments, such as oiling.
For a bigger purchase, ask for a gem report (for example from an independent lab) and check the return policy.
UK hallmarking on the gold or silver is worth looking for.

Where to buy in the UK

These came up in my search. Some of the sources are the retailers’ own marketing, so treat their praise accordingly and check independent reviews.

Lily Arkwright: UK hallmarked, with Chatham lab-grown emerald rings. Chatham is one of the best-known lab emerald producers, and reviews on the page are positive.

Astrid & Miyu: A UK brand that launched a lab-grown collection featuring emeralds and pink sapphires in solid 14k gold. This is a good option for everyday, more affordable pieces.

Angara (UK site): Offers both natural and lab-grown emerald jewellery, with pieces starting from £599.

Juvetti: A London brand that sells loose stones as well as jewellery, such as an oval lab-grown emerald for £130. That’s useful if you want a custom setting.

Hatton Garden jewellers: Several of them will source lab emeralds and make a custom setting. Prices and stock vary, so ask for the stone’s origin and treatment in writing.
