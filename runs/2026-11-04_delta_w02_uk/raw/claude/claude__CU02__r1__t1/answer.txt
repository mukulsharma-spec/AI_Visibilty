Free engraving on gemstone rings is less common than on wedding bands, and the offers I found were limited.

Holts Gems (Hatton Garden, online) is the best fit. Its gemstone ring pages, such as the Ruby Fern, include an “Add Engraving” option with a 38-character maximum and a “Free Engraving from Holts” banner. Its site also says free engraving on engagement rings and wedding bands is available when you subscribe, online only, with the code ENGRAVING at checkout (terms apply). The wording of the two offers differs, so check which one covers your ring before you order.

Fossil lists engraving as free on its Ellis signet ring. That one is stainless steel rather than a gemstone ring, so it’s only useful if you’re open to fashion jewellery.

Some stores I’d treat with caution:

The Diamond Store offers laser engraving on selected platinum and 18K gold rings, but its help page says each engravable ring shows the applicable fee, so it isn’t free.

Jewlr and Astrid & Miyu both offer engraving, but I found nothing saying it’s free.
Lunns (Northern Ireland) ran a free engraving offer, but it was a limited-time August 2023 promotion on engagement and wedding rings, so it’s probably not current.

Free engraving offers are often seasonal or tied to specific ranges, and they change often. Before buying, check the checkout page for a £0 engraving line, or email the store to confirm. High-street jewellers such as H. Samuel, Ernest Jones and Goldsmiths are worth asking too. I couldn’t confirm their current policies from my search.
