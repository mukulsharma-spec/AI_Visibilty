Yes, it can be safe. The risk is mostly in how you buy, not in buying online as such. UK consumer law actually works in your favour.

What protects you

Cancellation rights: Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations, you generally get 14 days after delivery to cancel an online purchase. The big exception is made-to-order or personalised pieces, so a custom-set ring may not be returnable. Check this before you pay.
Section 75: If you pay by credit card, the card company is jointly liable for purchases between £100 and £30,000. Debit cards and PayPal “friends and family” payments offer weaker protection.
Hallmarking: In the UK, gold and platinum items over a small weight threshold must be hallmarked, so check the ring’s metal is hallmarked.

What to check on the sapphire itself

Treatment disclosure: Most sapphires are heat-treated, which is normal and accepted. Unheated stones cost significantly more, and some treatments (like beryllium diffusion) should be disclosed and priced lower. A seller who can’t tell you clearly is a red flag.
Natural vs lab-grown: Both are legitimate but priced very differently, so make sure the listing states which it is.
Independent certificate: For a significant stone, look for a report from a respected lab such as GIA, SSEF, Gübelin, GRS or AGL. Origin (Ceylon, Burma, Kashmir and so on) only matters to price if a lab confirms it. Retailer-issued “certificates” aren’t independent.
Photos and video: Sapphire colour is everything, and it varies a lot with lighting. Ask for video in natural light, or choose a seller that lets you see the exact stone.

Where to buy

Established UK high-street and online jewellers: Beaverbrooks, Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones and Mappin & Webb are convenient, with straightforward returns and aftercare. Prices are often higher, and the sapphire selection is usually narrower.
Online specialists: Angara and Blue Nile (both have UK-facing sites) offer large sapphire ranges and clear return policies. Check current delivery terms, and for any US-based site, whether import VAT or duty will apply.
Independent and Hatton Garden jewellers: These are good for choosing a specific stone, bespoke settings and better value. Look for membership of a trade body like the National Association of Jewellers, and for a real shopfront or workshop and a long track record.
Gem-focused dealers: If you want a high-quality or unheated stone, look for dealers who sell lab-certified gems and have been trading for years.

Red flags: prices far below comparable stones, pressure to pay by bank transfer, no clear returns policy, vague treatment information, and reviews that look suspiciously uniform.

Finally, insure the ring once you have it, and get an independent valuation if it’s a high-value purchase.
