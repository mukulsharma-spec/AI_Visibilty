It depends on your budget and whether you want natural or lab-created, but these are the places I’d look in the UK.

Best overall for choice and value: Hatton Garden independents (London). Dealers here sell loose rubies and set them in custom rings, usually for less than high-street prices. You can negotiate and ask for lab reports. Check Google reviews and ask about their returns policy first, as standards vary.

High-street chains (easier, but pricier): Beaverbrooks, Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, and Mappin & Webb. They offer good aftercare and clear returns, but the rubies are often small and heavily treated, and the markup is higher. Mappin & Webb and Boodles are better if you want a high-end piece.

Online specialists: Angara UK and Blue Nile (international shipping) have wide selections of natural rubies with detailed grading info. Online you get lots of choice and a 14-day cancellation right under UK distance-selling law, though you can’t see the colour in person.

Antique and vintage: Auction houses like Bonhams or Sotheby’s for top-end pieces, or reputable antique dealers in Bond Street or Camden Passage. You can find real character and good value, but make sure the ring has a hallmark and, ideally, a valuation.

What to check before buying:

- Treatment: Most rubies are heat-treated, which is normal and acceptable, but it should be disclosed. Untreated stones cost far more.
- Origin and colour: Burmese stones fetch premiums, but Mozambique and Thai rubies can be excellent value. Look for a vivid red with slight blue, often called “pigeon’s blood”.
- Certification: For anything over around £1,000, ask for a report from GIA, SSEF, or Gübelin.
- Lab-created rubies: These are chemically identical and much cheaper, a good option if budget matters, but they should be clearly labelled.
- Hallmark: Gold and platinum should carry a UK hallmark.
