Here’s what matters for a gemstone ring in the UK, plus what I’d need to narrow it down.

Where to go

  • An independent jeweller with an in-house workshop is usually best. Many shop-floor jewellers just post rings off, which adds time and cost.
  • Look for members of the National Association of Jewellers (NAJ) or a goldsmith in a jewellery hub such as Hatton Garden in London or the Jewellery Quarter in Birmingham.
  • If you bought the ring from a retailer, ask whether they offer free or discounted resizing and engraving. Some do within a set period.

The stone is the main risk
Resizing involves heat, and some gems don’t tolerate it. Opal, emerald, pearl, turquoise, tanzanite, and sometimes amethyst or topaz can crack, discolour, or fade. A good jeweller will either remove the stone first or use lower-heat or laser methods. Diamonds, sapphires, and rubies are far more robust. Ask them directly how they’ll protect your stone.

Resizing limits

  • Going up or down one to two sizes is generally straightforward. Beyond that, the design or setting can be distorted.
  • Eternity rings, channel-set stones, and rings with stones all around the band are hard or impossible to resize.
  • Tungsten, titanium, and ceramic bands usually can’t be resized at all.

Engraving

  • Do it after resizing, as the jeweller may need to polish the band afterward.
  • Inside the band is standard. Laser engraving is crisp and works on thinner bands, while hand engraving looks more traditional.
  • It must be placed so it doesn’t damage the hallmark.
  • Check how many characters fit, as it depends on band width.

Practicalities

  • Get a written quote, a turnaround time (often a few days to two weeks), and confirmation that the ring is insured while in their care.
  • Photograph the ring beforehand.
  • If you’re posting it, use tracked, insured delivery.
  • Costs vary widely with metal and complexity. Simple resizing in gold or silver is often modest, while platinum or complex settings cost more, so get a couple of quotes.
