Short answer: there’s no universal standard. Grades like AA, AAA and AAAA are trade or marketing labels, not scientific or regulated measurements, so one seller’s AAA can be another seller’s AA. They’re most meaningful as a comparison within a single retailer’s range.

What they generally try to convey

Most sellers use the letters to summarise a mix of the “4 Cs” for coloured stones:

Colour: the most important factor. Higher grades mean more vivid, saturated, evenly distributed colour.
Clarity: fewer visible inclusions. Higher grades look cleaner to the naked eye.
Cut: better proportions and symmetry, so the stone sparkles rather than looking dull or “windowed”.
Carat weight: usually the least important to the grade itself.

Roughly, the pattern tends to run like this (varies by seller):

A / AA: lower grade, noticeable inclusions, paler or uneven colour. Budget end.
AAA: good commercial quality, attractive colour, minor inclusions.
AAAA: the top of a retailer’s range, strong colour and clarity. Sometimes used for “gem quality”.

Why to be cautious

The scale is unregulated, so a seller can use AAAA freely, and it’s often used loosely on cheaper stones.
Gem types differ. What counts as “eye-clean” for an emerald (usually heavily included) is very different from an aquamarine or topaz.
Treatments matter more than the letters. Heating, oil or resin filling, dyeing and irradiation are common and affect value and durability. A good seller discloses these.

UK-specific tips

Ask for a gemmological report for anything pricey. Respected labs include the Gem-A lab (formerly the Gem-A Gem Testing Laboratory, based in London), SSEF, GIA and Gübelin. A lab report is far more reliable than a letter grade.
Check disclosure. Under UK consumer law (Consumer Rights Act 2015), items must be as described, so treatments and whether a stone is natural or synthetic should be stated clearly.
Hallmarking is separate. The UK Assay Office hallmark applies to the precious metal (gold, silver, platinum), not the gemstone, so it tells you nothing about stone quality.
Look for Gem-A members or FGA-qualified gemmologists (Fellow of the Gemmological Association) if you want expert advice.
Use returns. Distance selling rules give you a 14-day cancellation window for online purchases, which is useful for judging colour in person.

Practical advice
