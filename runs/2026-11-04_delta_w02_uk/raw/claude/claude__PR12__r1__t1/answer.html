For an unheated sapphire at that price, the lab report matters as much as the seller. I’d focus on these routes:

UK-based sellers

Hatton Garden dealers (London): Specialist coloured-stone dealers here can source unheated stones and let you view them in person. Ask to see reports and request a stone on approval, which reputable dealers often allow. Auction houses: Bonhams, Christie’s, Sotheby’s, and regional houses like Fellows (Birmingham) regularly sell unheated Ceylon, Burmese, and Kashmir sapphires with lab reports. Prices can be good, but factor in buyer’s premium (often 25%+) and VAT, and note that sales are usually final. High-end jewellers such as Graff or Boodles carry exceptional stones, but you’ll pay a significant retail premium.

Overseas dealers who ship to the UK

Gem Rock Auctions, Lotus Gemology, and Sri Lankan or Thai dealers often offer better value, especially for Ceylon sapphires. Factor in 20% import VAT and shipping insurance.

What to insist on

A report from a top lab (Gübelin, SSEF, GRS, or AGL) stating “no indications of heating.” Lesser labs are more lenient, and many “unheated” claims don’t hold up. At $10,000+, a report is non-negotiable. Origin on the report. Sri Lanka, Madagascar, Kashmir, and Burma command different premiums, and Kashmir and Burma cost far more. Return terms in writing. Distance sales give you a 14-day cancellation right in the UK, but confirm it applies, and pay by credit card to get Section 75 protection. Independent verification. Gem-A or an independent gemmologist can check the stone before your return window ends.

Inventory and policies change often, so verify each seller’s current terms before buying. If you tell me your preferred colour, size, and budget, I can help narrow down what to look for and what’s reasonable to pay.
