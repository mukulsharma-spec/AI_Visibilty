May’s birthstone is the emerald, a rich green stone that’s a lovely choice for a mum. Some thoughts to help you narrow things down:

Types of jewellery that work well

  • Pendant necklace: A single emerald on a fine gold or silver chain is classic and easy to wear every day.
  • Stud or drop earrings: Small emerald studs are timeless, and drops feel a bit more special for occasions.
  • Ring: Lovely if you know her size and style, but riskier as a gift without that.
  • Bracelet: A delicate chain bracelet with an emerald accent is a nice middle ground.

Budget guide (roughly)

  • Under £100: Lab-created emerald or emerald-green simulants in sterling silver or gold vermeil. These look great and are often very good quality.
  • £100-£300: Natural emeralds in small sizes set in 9ct gold or silver.
  • £300+: Larger or higher-grade natural emeralds in 9ct or 18ct gold, often with diamond accents.

Good to know

  • Emeralds are softer and more brittle than most gemstones, so a necklace or earrings will hold up better than a ring she’d wear daily.
  • Natural emeralds almost always have tiny inclusions, which is normal and part of their character.
  • Lab-created emeralds are real emeralds chemically, with fewer flaws and a lower price.
  • If you want gold, check whether it’s solid gold (9ct, 18ct), gold vermeil, or just gold plated, as quality and price vary a lot.

Where to look in the UK<br>Gemstone-focused jewellers like Angara, Ernest Jones, H. Samuel, Goldsmiths, and Beaverbrooks are good starting points, and Etsy has lots of independent makers if you want something more unique or personalised.

To point you toward something more specific, it would help to know your rough budget and whether she prefers gold, silver, or rose gold, and whether her style is more classic, modern, or vintage.
