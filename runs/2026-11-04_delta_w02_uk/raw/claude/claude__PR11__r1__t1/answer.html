To be sure the sapphires are real:

Stick to gold or platinum settings. Sapphire is a precious stone, so real ones are almost always set in gold. Cheap “sapphire” earrings at £10–£30 are usually glass, synthetic spinel or lab-created stones, even when the headline says sapphire.
Check the listing wording. Look for “natural” or “genuine” sapphire. If it says “created”, “simulated” or “lab”, it isn’t a mined stone. Lab-grown sapphire is chemically real but much cheaper, so make sure it’s what you’re paying for.
Expect heat treatment. Nearly all sapphires at this price are heat-treated, which is normal and accepted in the trade. Retailers should disclose it, so ask if the listing doesn’t say.
Look for a hallmark. In the UK, gold above 1g must be hallmarked, and 9ct shows as 375 and 18ct as 750. The hallmark covers the metal only, not the stones, so it won’t prove the sapphire is real.
Buy from someone with returns. A 14–30 day return window lets you take the earrings to an independent jeweller or a gemmologist to confirm the stones.
