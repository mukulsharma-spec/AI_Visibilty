It depends on what you’re buying and how much you’re spending, but as a rule: for anything where the gemstone itself is the value (a sapphire engagement ring, a good emerald), buy from a jeweller. For inexpensive, fashion-style pieces, Amazon is fine if you pick carefully.

Why a jeweller is usually safer for real gemstones

You can see the stone in person. Colour, cut and clarity vary a lot between stones with the same name, and photos are unreliable.

Reputable UK jewellers will disclose treatments (heat treatment, fracture filling, irradiation, etc.), which matter a lot to value and are often glossed over online.

You can ask for an independent grading or identification report, or at least a proper receipt describing the stone.

Hallmarking is easier to verify. In the UK, precious metal items above certain weight thresholds legally have to be hallmarked (gold, silver, platinum and palladium), so check for the hallmark.

Returns and after-sales (resizing, repairs, valuation for insurance) are simpler with a physical shop.

Look for membership of the National Association of Jewellers (NAJ) or the Fine Jewellery... and for Gem-A qualified staff if you’re spending serious money.

Where Amazon can work

Lower-cost pieces, such as simple silver or gold-plated jewellery with semi-precious stones, where you’re paying for the look rather than investment value.

Sold and dispatched by Amazon itself, or by a well-reviewed established jeweller using Amazon as a storefront.

The UK Consumer Rights Act and Amazon’s returns policy give you decent protection, so you can send it back if it disappoints.

Risks on Amazon

Many listings use vague wording like “created”, “simulated”, “lab-grown” or just “sapphire-style” without making the stone’s nature obvious. Read the description carefully.

Third-party marketplace sellers vary widely, and fake or misdescribed items do get through.

Reviews can be unreliable or incentivised.

Metal purity claims (“925”, “9ct”) should still be backed by a hallmark for anything above the legal weight limits, and a lot of cheap pieces are plated base metal.

A middle route

Specialist online jewellers (with their own websites, clear grading information, certificates and returns policies) often offer better prices than high-street shops while being more trustworthy than a random marketplace seller. Check for reviews on independent sites like Trustpilot, and confirm the 14-day cooling-off return right for distance sales, which applies to online purchases in the UK.

Quick rule of thumb

Under roughly £100, for fashion jewellery: Amazon is reasonable.

£100 to £500: buy from a established online jeweller or local shop, ask for stone details and treatment disclosure.

Over £500, or anything special like an engagement ring: see it in person, get a certificate or valuation, and buy from a jeweller you trust.
