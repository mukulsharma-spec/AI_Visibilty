Several UK-facing online jewellers say they provide independent lab reports with coloured stones, but it’s rarely automatic across their whole range.

- Sonny’s Jewellers says it always provides a certificate from a reputable gemmological laboratory when you buy gemstones from them.
- Reve Diamonds says every stone in its certified gemstones section comes with a certificate of authenticity covering its characteristics and grading. You can also pair a stone with a custom setting.
- Berry’s Jewellers lists pieces such as a natural pink sapphire and diamond pendant described as GIA certificated.
- Timothy Roe labels individual pieces “with Certificate”, including ruby and emerald rings. Its own guide explains that coloured stones are often sold without reports, so it varies by piece.
- Holts Gems has in-house gemmologists qualified through Gem-A or GIA, and says identified stones come with a certificate covering cut, colour, weight and measurements. This may be a lab-style identification rather than a report from one of the major labs.
- Laurelle Antique Jewellery has partnered with European Diamond and Gemstone Reports and the Birmingham Assay Office for gemstone certificates, so it’s worth checking if you like antique pieces.

A few things to check before you buy:

- Who issued the report? Reports from GIA, Gübelin, SSEF, AGL or similar are the most widely recognised. Gem-A, IGI and UK labs are also used. An in-house “certificate” is less independent than a lab report.
- Does it cover origin and treatment? For rubies, sapphires and emeralds, whether the stone is heated or oil-treated, and whether the origin is stated, has a large effect on value. A basic identification report won’t always say.
- Is it per stone? Many retailers only supply reports on higher-value stones, so confirm the specific item has one, ideally listed on the product page before you order.
- Can you verify it? Most major labs have online report lookups where you can enter the report number.
