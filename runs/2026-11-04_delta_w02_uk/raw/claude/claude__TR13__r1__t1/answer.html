Reviews of the established online sellers are mostly positive, but the complaints that do appear are consistent.

Most of the easy-to-find praise is on seller-run testimonial pages or on sites where the reviews read as generic, so I’d give more weight to Trustpilot, Feefo and independent forums such as Pricescope. Treat the sample as thin and somewhat skewed.

What buyers praise

Returns: one sapphire buyer said they were nervous about such a big purchase online but were reassured by the return policy. [thenaturalsapphirecompany]

Photos and video: another buyer had checked about 12 websites and ordered samples from several retailers. They preferred the one that sent multiple photos and videos in different lighting. [thenaturalsapphirecompany]

Service and packaging: the Feefo reviews for Gem Collector UK mostly mention good gemstones, packaging and delivery. Gem Collector UK is a UK seller. [feefo]

Prices from overseas: GemSelect reviewers said shipping charges from overseas were reasonable and customer service was excellent. [resellerratings]

What goes wrong

Size: on Gemondo’s Trustpilot page, some people were dissatisfied because certain items looked much smaller in person than online. [trustpilot]

Colour versus photos: this is the classic complaint. One forum reviewer described nicely photographed stones that were almost black in person, with the same photo reused for many stones. Buying guides add that camera settings, background, lighting, screen brightness and editing can all change how a stone looks. Colour-change stones are reportedly very difficult to buy online. [www.pricescope.com +2]

Treatments: heating is routine for sapphires, but price depends on it. One seller says heated and unheated sapphire can differ in price by a factor of five or more. Some forum users claim many Thai sellers admit to heating but not to fracture filling. [skyjems] [pricescope]

Cancelled orders: one Gem Collector UK reviewer said they twice ordered loose gemstones that were accepted, then got emails about the gems. The review is cut off, so I can’t say why. [feefo]

Refund disputes: the usual forum advice is to file a claim with your credit card company or PayPal if the vendor won’t refund. [pricescope]

UK-specific: buying from overseas
Sources conflict, and older forum posts quote outdated VAT rates. GemSelect says standard Royal Mail shipments aren’t usually subject to customs tax, but express shipping incurs VAT plus a courier admin fee. Another guide says loose gemstones carry no duty but normally incur standard-rate VAT on import. One UK buyer reported £32.11 in charges on a £178 order shipped by FedEx, though that was jewellery supplies rather than gemstones. Check the current rules on gov.uk and ask the seller which shipping method they use before you order. [Do I have to pay import duty or tax on gemstones I buy from GemSelect? +2]

Practical takeaways
- Ask for video in different lighting.
- Get the treatment status in writing.
- Ask for a lab report on anything expensive.
- Confirm the return window.
- Pay by card or PayPal.
- Work out the total cost including shipping and any import charges.
