Generally, a specialist is the better bet for colored gemstones, though it depends on what you’re buying and how much you’re spending.

Why specialists usually win

Knowledge and selection. Colored stones vary enormously in quality (color saturation, clarity, cut, origin, treatment), and a specialist can explain why two sapphires of the same size differ hugely in price. Chains often have limited stock and staff who are trained in sales more than gemology.
Treatment disclosure. Most colored stones are treated in some way: heat for sapphires and rubies, oil for emeralds, and so on. Heat is standard and acceptable, but other treatments (fracture filling, diffusion, glass filling) significantly reduce value. A good specialist will tell you plainly what’s been done.
Better value for quality. Chains carry a large retail markup and tend to stock commercial-grade stones. A specialist can often source a better stone for the same money, or the same stone for less.
Custom work. If you want a bespoke piece, you can choose a loose stone and have it set.
Lab reports. For higher-value stones, specialists are more likely to offer independent certification from labs such as Gem-A, SSEF, Gübelin, or GIA.

When a chain is fine

Lower-budget pieces, say under a few hundred pounds, where you’re paying for the design rather than the stone’s quality.
Fashion or everyday jewellery where you don’t mind commercial-grade stones.
You value easy returns, in-store servicing, and a familiar brand.
Lab-created stones, where the quality differences matter less and pricing is more transparent.

UK-specific tips

Look for membership of the National Association of Jewellers (NAJ) or the Gemmological Association of Great Britain (Gem-A), and check whether the seller has a qualified gemmologist (FGA or DGA after their name).
Ask for a written description stating whether the stone is natural or synthetic and what treatments it has had, plus a lab report for anything expensive.
Hallmarking applies to the precious metal, not the gemstone, so it tells you nothing about the stone’s quality.
Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, goods must be as described and of satisfactory quality. Distance purchases (online) also give you a 14-day cancellation right, though bespoke items are generally exempt.
Hatton Garden in London is a famous hub for specialists, but standards vary there as anywhere, so apply the same checks. Reputable independent gem dealers exist across the country too.

Questions worth asking any seller

Is it natural or lab-grown?
What treatments has it had?
Can I see a lab report?
What’s your return and resizing policy?
Can I have it independently valued?
