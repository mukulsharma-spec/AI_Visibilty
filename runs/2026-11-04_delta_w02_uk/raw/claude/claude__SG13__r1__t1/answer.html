There are good options at most budgets in the UK, so here is how I’d split it up.

Fine jewellery (real gold and gemstones)

Dinny Hall sells the Libby cocktail ring in 9k gold. The green amethyst version is £1,400 with free UK delivery, with a 12mm x 8mm centre stone. There is also a blue topaz version.

Catherine Best makes bold, multi-stone colour rings. Cocktail Hour II uses topaz, blue and orange sapphire and peridot in 18ct yellow gold, though that one shows as out of stock. It’s worth checking their other styles.

Astley Clarke has a statement jewellery edit of cocktail rings, including demi-fine styles with coloured gemstones.

McCaul offers handmade cocktail rings in 18-carat gold or platinum set with coloured gemstones, with a London store you can visit.

Fenton has a limited-edition cocktail collection made from coloured gemstones, larger than their core rings.

Mid-range (silver or vermeil with large stones)

Notonthehighstreet has handmade British pieces, such as a rose gold vermeil on sterling silver ring with a large baguette-cut labradorite.

Lat Elita sells silver cocktail rings in blue topaz and ruby, with free UK shipping on orders over £99.

Vintage and secondhand

1stDibs lists many cocktail rings from London and UK sellers, including an 18k yellow gold ring with a 7.50ct emerald-cut citrine. These are usually high-end pieces.

Vinted is the cheap end, with costume statement rings from roughly £3 to £22. It’s good for trying a look, but quality varies.

If you want a real gemstone but not a fine-jewellery price, silver or vermeil with topaz, amethyst or citrine usually gives the biggest stone for the money.
