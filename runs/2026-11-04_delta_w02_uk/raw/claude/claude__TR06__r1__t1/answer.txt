I found a few UK-linked sellers with documented disclosure practices, but I couldn’t verify how every retailer handles it.

Sellers with clear policies

Apsara / Top Gem (UK): Its stated policy is full disclosure of any treatment applied to a gem. It assumes all rubies and sapphires have been heat treated unless it has evidence otherwise, and marks heated stones with (H). That default assumption is a good sign, since it errs toward telling you. [topgem]

Gems and Jewels UK (Birmingham, via 1stDibs): Its listings are mostly sapphires labelled untreated or unheated, often with GIA, GRA or IGI certification. That covers untreated stones well, but check how they describe anything that is treated. [1stdibs]

Prins & Prins: It publishes a guide stating that ethical jewellers always disclose heat treatment, and that fracture-filled stones and beryllium infusion require full disclosure. That shows its attitude, but I didn’t confirm how its individual listings read. [prinsandprins]

Other places to check
Mainstream UK sellers such as Gemporia, and international ones that ship to the UK such as Angara, generally state treatments in their listings. I haven’t verified that here, so check their product pages yourself.

How to judge any seller

Look for a lab report: A report from a neutral third party records what the stone is and what has been done to it. GIA, SSEF, Gübelin, and in the UK Gem-A are well regarded. [skyjems]

Ask directly: Does the listing say whether the stone is treated and what the treatment is? The questions to ask are whether it has been treated, what treatment was applied, and whether it is permanent or needs maintenance. [prinsandprins]

Be wary of “natural” with no further detail: Most sapphires online are described simply as “natural” without proper treatment disclosure. Natural doesn’t mean untreated. [thenaturalsapphirecompany]

Know which stones are usually treated: Corundum (ruby and sapphire) is the most commonly heated family, and citrine, tanzanite and aquamarine are also often heated. Heat treatment is widely accepted and doesn’t make a stone fake, but it should affect the price. Fracture or glass filling is more of a concern, since the filler can be damaged by heat or solvents. [wildfawnjewellery]

Check the returns policy: It should let you return the stone if an independent lab finds an undisclosed treatment.

What stone are you looking for? Disclosure norms differ a lot between sapphire, emerald and tanzanite, so I can suggest what to look for and what price to expect.
