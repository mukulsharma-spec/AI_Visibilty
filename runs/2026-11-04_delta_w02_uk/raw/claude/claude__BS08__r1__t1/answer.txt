There are plenty of UK options, and the right one depends mostly on budget and metal.

Budget (under about £100)

TJC sells sterling silver halo studs with aquamarine and zircon for about £50–60. These use natural zircon rather than diamonds in the halo, so they suit casual, everyday wear.

Mid-range (about £500–£900), solid gold with diamond halos

Hugh Rice has a round aquamarine and diamond halo stud in 18ct white gold for £595. They also do a pear-cut pair, listed at £850.
The Fine Jewellery Company has a 9ct white gold pair with 6mm round stones for £585, with a British hallmark.
Robert Gatward Jewellers has an 18ct white gold pear-cut pair, 0.57ct aquamarine with 0.19ct diamonds, for £765.

Higher-end (about £1,400–£2,900)

Diamond Rocks has round, oval and emerald-cut halo studs in 18ct white gold. The oval pair is £1,440 and the emerald-cut pair (1.65ct aquamarine) is £1,920. Both come with UK hallmarking and free insured UK shipping.
KJJ has a round-cut pair in 18ct white gold for £1,495.
Banks Lyon has a 1.44ct pair in 18ct white gold for £2,500, with free next-day UK delivery and a 14-day returns policy.
Angara UK is the best choice if you want to choose stone size, grade and metal, including platinum. Prices run from about £1,559 for oval half-halo studs to £2,865 for larger cushion-cut ones. They also offer 0% interest payment plans.

What to check before buying

Metal and hallmark: 9ct is cheaper and more durable. 18ct has a richer look and is more common at the higher end. Look for a UK hallmark.
Stone colour: Aquamarine ranges from pale to a deeper sky blue. The deeper tones cost more, so look closely at photos or ask for extra images.
Returns: Check the policy, because colour can look different in person.
Diamond halo vs. zircon: Diamond halos usually mean more sparkle and a higher price.
