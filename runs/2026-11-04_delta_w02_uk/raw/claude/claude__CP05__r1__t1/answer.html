Tanzanite is a beautiful choice, but it comes with real trade-offs for a ring worn every day.

The main concern is durability. Tanzanite is about 6.5 to 7 on the Mohs scale, which is noticeably softer than diamond (10), sapphire (9) or even most quartz. Everyday life will gradually scratch it, and it also has perfect cleavage in one direction, meaning a hard knock on the wrong angle can chip or split it. An engagement ring takes more knocks than most jewellery, since it’s on your hand for door handles, bags, gym equipment and so on.

What it has going for it:

A striking blue-violet colour that’s quite different from the usual sapphire or diamond
Trichroic, so it shifts between blue, violet and sometimes burgundy depending on the light and angle
Rare, since it’s only mined in one small area of Tanzania, which also makes it more of a meaningful, distinctive choice
Generally much cheaper than sapphire of similar size and colour

If you go for it, these things help:

Choose a protective setting. A bezel or halo setting shields the edges far better than a classic claw setting. Avoid very exposed, high-set designs.
Pick a shape with fewer vulnerable points. Ovals, rounds and cushion cuts tend to be safer than pear, marquise or emerald cuts, whose corners and edges are the weak spots.
Consider a smaller or deeper stone rather than a large, shallow one.
Take it off for gym, cleaning, gardening and anything knocky. Skip ultrasonic cleaners and steam; warm soapy water and a soft brush are safest.
Insure it. In the UK, check whether home contents cover is enough or whether you need a specified items policy, and get a valuation certificate.

Buying tips for the UK:

Most tanzanite on the market is heat-treated to bring out the blue. That’s standard and accepted, but a reputable seller will tell you. Natural unheated stones are rare and very expensive.
Look for a lab report (e.g. GIA, or a UK lab such as the Gem-A or SSEF/Gübelin for higher-value stones) on anything significant.
Buy from a jeweller who is clear about treatment, origin and return policy. Under the Consumer Rights Act you have protections around goods matching their description, and many UK jewellers offer a 14-day cancellation window for distance sales.
Colour matters most to price: deep, saturated blue-violet commands the premium, while paler or greyish stones are cheaper.

Bottom line: it’s a good choice if you love the colour and are happy to treat the ring with a bit of care, especially in a protective setting. If you want something you can wear and forget about, a sapphire (which has a similar blue-violet range, and some lovely options in that colour) or a diamond-set design with a tanzanite accent might suit better.

Is the ring for you or for someone else? And do you have a rough budget? That would help narrow down shapes and settings.
