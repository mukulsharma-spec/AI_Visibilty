Congratulations on 45 years! That’s a sapphire anniversary, so it’s a fitting gift. Where to shop depends mostly on budget and on whether you want something new, vintage, or custom.

High-street and national jewellers. These are the easiest places to start if you want to see pieces in person and have easy returns. Goldsmiths sells sapphire rings online and in stores across the UK, and Ernest Jones and H. Samuel both carry sapphire ranges. H. Samuel’s start in the low hundreds in 9ct gold, while Ernest Jones has 18ct and platinum pieces at higher prices. Berry’s is a step up, with its own collections in 18ct gold and platinum, and over 125 years of experience. These shops are convenient, but the stone quality is often modest at the lower end, so ask what treatments the sapphire has had.

Independent and specialist jewellers. For a better stone, one specialist advises visiting a gemstone specialist rather than a general high-street chain, particularly for vintage or antique pieces. Hatton Garden in London is the traditional hub. For example, The Diamond Ring Company sources its stones directly, offers private appointments, and lets you try pieces before buying. Mozeris Fine Antiques, which offers viewings by appointment in London and Essex plus insured delivery across the UK, is another option if you like the idea of an antique piece.

Auctions. For a 45th anniversary, an antique or estate piece can be a lovely, characterful choice and often better value. Fellows, Dawsons and Noonans all run regular jewellery sales, some online. Use a reputable house that provides condition reports, and keep in mind that auction pieces usually need more due diligence on your part.

A caution. C.W. Sellors, a Derbyshire jeweller that appears in many sapphire searches, had administrators appointed on 13 January 2026. I’d avoid ordering from them without checking their current status first.

Whoever you buy from:

Ask whether the sapphire is natural, and whether it is heat-treated. Heat treatment is very common and fine, but it should be disclosed and priced accordingly.
For a significant stone, ask for a gemmological report from a lab such as GIA, SSEF or Gübelin.
Check the piece is UK hallmarked, and look at the return policy. Most reputable sellers offer 30 days.
Ask about resizing before you buy, or find out her size discreetly.
