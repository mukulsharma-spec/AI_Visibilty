There are a few good routes in the UK, depending on your budget and how much you want to customise.

Online jewellers with real gemstones

C W Sellors describes itself as one of the UK’s leading diamond and gemstone specialists, and has a dedicated promise ring collection. It’s a good middle-to-upper option.

Austen & Blake sells promise rings and mentions bespoke customisation options with UK delivery. It suits a more premium, diamond-led ring.

Jewlr UK has promise rings starting at £99, including engravable gemstone styles. It’s good for a lower budget, but check whether the stone is natural or lab-grown, and check the metal.

Holts Gems is worth a look for coloured gemstones.

Other options

High street: Beaverbrooks, Ernest Jones, H. Samuel and Goldsmiths let you see and try rings in person, which helps with sizing.
Independent jewellers: Hatton Garden in London, the Jewellery Quarter in Birmingham, and local independents are often best for custom work and for asking questions face to face.
Etsy UK: good for handmade or vintage pieces, but check seller reviews, hallmarks and returns carefully.

What to check before you buy

Hallmarks. In the UK, gold, silver and platinum items above a small weight threshold must carry an Assay Office hallmark. This is your independent proof of metal purity.
Stone durability. Sapphire, ruby and diamond are very hard and suit daily wear. Emerald is prone to chipping, and opal and pearl are soft and scratch easily.
Natural vs lab-grown. Both are “real” gemstones, but lab-grown stones cost much less. Retailers should state clearly which one you’re getting.
Setting. Low-set or bezel settings survive daily knocks better than tall claws.
Returns and resizing. Look for a decent returns window and free resizing, since ring size is the hardest thing to guess.
