A ruby is a lovely choice. It’s one of the hardest gemstones after diamond, so it suits daily wear. Here’s where to look in the UK, roughly by approach.

Independent jewellers and bespoke

Hatton Garden (London) and the Jewellery Quarter (Birmingham) are the best places for choice and value. You can see loose rubies in person, compare colour, and have a ring made around your chosen stone. Look for shops that are members of the National Association of Jewellers (NAJ) and can show you lab reports.
Bespoke is often the best route for rubies, because ready-made ruby engagement rings are much less common than diamond ones.

High street and established retailers

Goldsmiths, Mappin & Webb, Beaverbrooks, Ernest Jones, and Laings all stock ruby rings, though selection varies by branch. They’re good for easy returns and aftercare, but you’ll often pay a premium and have less choice on quality.
At the luxury end, Boodles, Garrard, and Graff work with fine rubies, with prices to match.

Online

Angara has a UK site with a large selection of ruby rings, including lab-created options.
Independent Hatton Garden sellers also sell online and will often send photos and videos on request.

Antique and vintage

Rubies were hugely popular in Victorian and Edwardian jewellery, and antique pieces can offer excellent value and character. Try specialist antique dealers, fairs, and auction houses. Make sure the ring is professionally checked and the stone is secure.

What to check before you buy

Treatment: Most rubies are heat-treated, which is normal and accepted, but it should be disclosed. Untreated stones are rare and far more expensive.
Natural vs lab-created: Lab-created rubies are chemically identical and much cheaper. Both are valid, but it must be clearly stated.
Colour: Look for a rich, vivid red. Too dark or too pinkish lowers the value.
Certification: For any significant stone, ask for a report from a lab like GIA, SSEF, or Gübelin. Smaller stones may come with a retailer’s valuation instead.
Hallmarking: In the UK, gold and platinum settings above a small weight must be hallmarked.
Returns: Online purchases usually have a 14-day cooling-off period, but made-to-order or bespoke pieces are typically excluded. Check this before ordering.

Roughly what’s your budget, and do you want natural or lab-created? That would help narrow down which route suits you best.
