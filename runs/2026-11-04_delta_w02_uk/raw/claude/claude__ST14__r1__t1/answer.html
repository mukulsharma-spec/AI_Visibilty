The catch is that both sources are selling to you, so check reviews elsewhere. angara

For example, a 0.20ct emerald half eternity ring was listed at £433.50 on sale. They’re worth comparing against each other, but “sale” prices from a “was” price are often inflated, so judge by the final price per carat and the stone’s quality. diamond-heaven
  • Juwelo: It says its emerald pieces come with a certificate of authenticity and sells at lower prices through direct production. It’s a good place to find bargains, but stone quality varies a lot, so read the descriptions closely. juwelo
  • Ramsdens: This UK high-street jeweller offers free standard UK delivery and 14-day returns. It’s a lower-risk option if you want a physical shop to fall back on. ramsdensjewellery

I’d avoid very cheap marketplace or deal-site listings (like £7 “emerald green” rings). Those are usually glass or crystal, not emerald.

How to avoid overpaying:

  • Almost all natural emeralds are treated with oil or resin. That’s normal, but the seller should disclose it, and heavier treatment should mean a lower price.
  • Ask for a lab report (such as GIA, SSEF or Gübelin) on any natural stone worth more than a few hundred pounds. Smaller, cheaper stones rarely come with one.
  • Look at the real photos of the stone you’re buying, not stock images.
  • Check the seller’s return policy before you buy. UK distance-selling rules give you a cooling-off period, but jewellers’ policies vary.
