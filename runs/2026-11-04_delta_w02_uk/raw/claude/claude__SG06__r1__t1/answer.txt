It depends on your budget and whether you want natural or lab-created stones, but here are good options in the UK by tier.

Specialist gemstone jewellers (online)

Angara has a UK site with a wide range of sapphire, ruby, emerald and mixed-gemstone tennis bracelets in different metals. It’s one of the better places to compare natural against lab-created stones.
Blue Nile UK is mostly diamonds but has some gemstone styles, with good detail on stone quality.

UK high-street and established chains

Beaverbrooks, Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones and Fraser Hart have solid ranges and let you see pieces in person. They tend to cost more than online specialists for similar stones.
H. Samuel and Pandora suit lower budgets, though the stones are usually small or lab-created.

For better value or something custom

Hatton Garden independents in London can make or source a bracelet to your spec, often at a better price than big retailers. Stick to well-reviewed shops and ask for itemised stone details.
Auction houses and antique dealers are good if you like vintage or Art Deco styles.

What to check before buying

Natural, lab-created or treated: Many sapphires and emeralds are heat- or oil-treated, which is normal but should be disclosed. This affects both price and value.
Hallmark: In the UK, gold, silver and platinum items over a minimum weight must be hallmarked, so look for it.
Setting and clasp: Look for secure claw or bezel settings and a box clasp with a safety catch. Tennis bracelets are easy to lose if the clasp is weak.
Total carat weight: Compare this, not the per-stone size, since listings vary.
Returns: Online purchases get a 14-day cancellation right in the UK, but check whether the retailer offers more, especially for resizing.
