Here are the main routes in the UK, depending on what you want.

Independent and online specialists (usually best value)

- Hatton Garden, London: the traditional diamond district, with dozens of independent dealers where you can compare stones in person and negotiate.
- Taylor & Hart: well-regarded for custom engagement rings, with natural and lab-grown options.
- Angelic Diamonds, Vashi and Diamonds Factory: online-focused retailers with large stone selections and certified diamonds.
- Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth (UK sites): big online catalogues where you can filter by cut, colour and clarity.

High-street chains (more convenient, typically pricier)

- Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, Beaverbrooks, H. Samuel and Berry’s. They’re good for seeing rings in person, financing options and easy aftercare, though you usually pay more per carat.

What to check before buying

- Certification: insist on a GIA, IGI or HRD grading report for a 1 carat stone. Unlabelled or in-house grading can be inflated.
- Cut matters most: a well-cut stone with a lower colour or clarity grade (say G–H colour, VS2–SI1 clarity) often looks better than a poorly cut “higher grade” one.
- Natural vs lab-grown: lab-grown diamonds are chemically identical and often a fraction of the price. As a rough guide, a 1 carat natural ring might run from about £3,500 to well over £8,000, while lab-grown is often around £800 to £2,000, but check current prices as they move.
- Returns: online purchases generally carry a 14-day cancellation right under UK consumer law, but bespoke or custom-made rings may be exempt, so check the retailer’s policy first.
- Hallmarking: UK precious metal settings should carry an assay hallmark.
- Insurance: get the ring valued and insured once you buy it.
