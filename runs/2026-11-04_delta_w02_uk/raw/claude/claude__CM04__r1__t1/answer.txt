Kay isn’t an option for you. It’s a US retailer, and as far as I know it doesn’t ship to the UK. Its parent company, Signet, owns H.Samuel and Ernest Jones here, but those are different shops with different stock.

Blue Nile does ship to the UK. According to a third-party buying guide, Blue Nile acts as importer of record for UK customers, so VAT is included in the price, and shipping is free with a 30-day return window. That guide may be dated, so check the checkout page for the final price. One warning from UK Trustpilot reviews: one customer reported that a commercial invoice never reached the local UPS team for customs clearance, and the ring went back to the US. That seems to be the exception, but it’s worth knowing about before you order. [Blue Nile UK Guide +2]

For rubies specifically, Blue Nile’s loose stones are listed as “Heated”. That’s normal, since most rubies on the market are heat-treated, and it’s good that they disclose it. Some of their ready-made ruby rings also carry notes like “Resizing not available”, so check that before you fall for a particular one. [bluenile] [bluenile]

Is there a better place? Maybe, depending on what matters to you:

- Hatton Garden independents or a UK bespoke jeweller: you can see the stone in person, get the ring sized properly, and avoid customs risk. Ruby colour varies a lot, and photos don’t capture it well.
- Established UK chains (Goldsmiths, Mappin & Webb, Beaverbrooks): easier returns and servicing, though you’ll usually pay more for a comparable stone.
- Vintage or antique dealers: natural, often unheated or Burmese-origin rubies sometimes turn up here. Ask for a lab report.

Whoever you buy from, ask which treatments the ruby has had. For anything over about £1,500, a report from a lab like GIA, SSEF or Gübelin is worth having. Also check the metal is hallmarked, which is a legal requirement in the UK for gold and platinum above a minimum weight.

What’s your rough budget, and are you after a particular style? That would help narrow down whether Blue Nile or a local jeweller makes more sense.
