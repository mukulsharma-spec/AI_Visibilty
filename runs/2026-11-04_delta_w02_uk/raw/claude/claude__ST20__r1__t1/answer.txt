The ring’s look comes down to the stone grade, the cut and the metal, more than the shop. Here’s where I’d start in the UK and what to check.

Where to look

Lily Arkwright is a UK online jeweller that offers over 450 moissanite designs in platinum and 18ct white, yellow and rose gold. It describes itself as an authorised retailer of Charles & Colvard moissanite, which is the best-known moissanite brand, and its pages mention UK hallmarking and 0% interest-free payments. It’s the most established UK option I found.

IfShe UK also sells moissanite rings in the UK, though I only saw its own site reviews, so read independent reviews first.

Many “best brands” roundups online are affiliate content, so I wouldn’t treat them as unbiased. Check Trustpilot and Reddit’s r/Moissanite for recent reviews before buying.

What stops it looking fake

Colour: Choose a colourless grade. One guide says the standard is the D, E or F range, so it looks icy white in platinum or white gold. Cheaper stones can look slightly yellow or grey in some lights.

Cut: Moissanite throws more rainbow “fire” than diamond, which is what makes it obvious to some people. Step cuts like emerald and asscher give a “hall of mirrors” effect rather than crushed-ice sparkle, so they look closer to a diamond. A good round or oval also works well. 
awareness-avenue

Size: Keep it realistic. Moissanite weighs less than diamond, so a stone sold as 2ct looks a bit larger than a 2ct diamond. 1 to 1.5ct looks natural on most hands.
Metal: Buy solid 9ct or 18ct gold or platinum with a UK hallmark. Thin gold-plated silver is what often makes a ring look cheap.
Marketplace listings: Avoid very cheap ones. Rings on sites like Walmart are often gold-plated sterling silver at $20 to $120, and the plating and stone quality vary a lot.

Before you pay, check the return window, whether the stone grade is stated, and whether there’s a warranty or resizing option. Seeing the ring in daylight on arrival is the best test.
