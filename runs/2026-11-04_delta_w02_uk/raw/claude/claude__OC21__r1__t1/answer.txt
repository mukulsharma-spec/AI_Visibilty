Congratulations! Here’s where I’d look in the UK, roughly by budget and style.

Mid-range fine jewellery brands (around £100 to £800)

Monica Vinader, Astley Clarke and Missoma (their fine jewellery line) all do gemstone pendants, rings and earrings in gold or gold vermeil. They’re popular for push presents because the pieces are wearable every day.
Auree and Daisy London are good for simpler, more affordable pieces.

High street jewellers (wide range, easy to browse in person)

Goldsmiths, Beaverbrooks, Ernest Jones and F. Hinds carry a lot of sapphire, emerald, ruby and diamond pieces, and often have sales or interest-free credit.
Mappin & Webb and Boodles are the more premium end if the budget stretches.

Something more personal

Hatton Garden, London has dozens of independent jewellers who can make a custom piece, such as a ring set with her or the baby’s birthstone. Allow a few weeks, so start early.
Etsy and Not On The High Street have independent makers, but check reviews carefully and ask whether the stone is natural, lab-grown or simulated.
Antique and vintage dealers, whether at fairs or in places like Bond Street Antiques Centre, are great if she likes something with history.

Tips

A birthstone for the baby’s month, or her own, is a lovely meaningful touch.
Ask whether the stone is natural or lab-grown. Both are real gemstones, but the price and value differ a lot.
For anything pricey, ask for a certificate or valuation, and check the return policy and ring-sizing options.
If the delivery date is uncertain, many jewellers let you buy now and exchange later.
