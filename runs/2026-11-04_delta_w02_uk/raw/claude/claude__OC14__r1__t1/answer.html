Treating yourself is a good reason. The best place depends on budget and whether you want something ready-made or one-of-a-kind.

High street and established jewellers (easy, with returns and aftercare)

Goldsmiths, Mappin & Webb, Ernest Jones: reliable ranges of sapphires, emeralds, rubies and more, with sales staff you can try rings on with.
Beaverbrooks: a well-regarded family-run chain with good service.
Boodles, Annoushka, Kiki McDonough: higher-end British names, with Kiki McDonough especially known for coloured gemstones.

Online, with a wide choice and good value

Angara: a big choice of natural and lab-grown gemstones, and you can customise the setting.
Monica Vinader and Astley Clarke: more modest price points, with stylish everyday gemstone rings.

For something unique or better value

Hatton Garden, London: the centre of the UK jewellery trade, with lots of independent dealers and makers. You can often get better value and bespoke work, but it’s worth browsing a few shops before buying.
Independent makers on Etsy or at craft fairs: good for one-off designs. Check reviews and make sure pieces are hallmarked.
Antique and vintage dealers or auction houses: vintage gemstone rings often have character and quality you won’t find new at the same price.

Tips whichever route you choose

For a pricier stone, ask whether it’s natural or lab-grown and whether it’s been treated (heating is common and fine, but it should be disclosed).
Look for a certificate from a lab like Gem-A or GIA for more valuable stones, and check for a UK hallmark on the metal.
Online purchases generally have a 14-day return window under UK consumer law, so you can see it in person before committing.
