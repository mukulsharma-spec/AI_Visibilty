Here are good places to look in the UK, grouped by what you might be after.

High street chains with big online ranges

Beaverbrooks, Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, F.Hinds and H.Samuel all carry a wide range of sapphire rings, from budget to mid-range, with easy returns and in-store sizing if you want to try one on first.

Online specialists (more choice, often better value)

Angara has a large selection of natural and lab-created sapphires in many cuts and metals, with customisation.
Blue Nile is best for diamond-accented sapphire rings and detailed stone information.
Taylor & Hart and Ingle & Rhode are well regarded for engagement-style rings, with an ethical-sourcing focus.

Fashion and everyday gemstone jewellery

Monica Vinader, Astley Clarke and Missoma have lighter, more affordable gemstone rings, though the stones are smaller.

Higher-end or one-of-a-kind

Boodles and Garrard for luxury pieces.
Etsy UK and independent Hatton Garden jewellers for custom or vintage pieces. Check seller reviews and ask for stone details before buying.

Things to check before you buy

Natural vs lab-created: both are real sapphire, but lab-created is much cheaper. Make sure the listing says which it is.
Treatment: most natural sapphires are heat-treated, which is normal and should be disclosed. Untreated stones cost much more.
Certification: for a significant stone, look for a report from GIA, IGI or SSEF.
Hallmark: gold, silver and platinum should carry a UK hallmark.
Returns: under UK law you generally get 14 days to cancel online orders, but custom or made-to-order rings may be excluded, so check the policy.
