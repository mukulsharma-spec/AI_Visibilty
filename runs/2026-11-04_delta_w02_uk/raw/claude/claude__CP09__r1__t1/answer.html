Yes, generally a lot less. Unheated (natural, untreated) sapphires of good quality command a significant premium, often multiples of the price of a comparable heated stone, and the gap widens with size and quality.

Why the difference

Heating is by far the most common treatment in sapphires. Most sapphires on the market have been heated to improve colour and clarity, so a fine stone that hasn’t needed it is genuinely rare. Rarity drives the price.

Rough guide

- A top-quality unheated sapphire (especially from sources like Burma/Myanmar, Kashmir, or Ceylon/Sri Lanka, and ideally over a carat or two) can be priced several times higher than an equivalent heated one.
- At the lower end, the gap is smaller, and sometimes a heated stone is simply the sensible choice because unheated stones in that quality range are either unavailable or only priced higher because of the label.
- Not all heating is equal. Standard heating is accepted and stable. Be wary of “beryllium diffusion” or other lattice or surface diffusion, and of glass-filled or fracture-filled stones, which are worth far less and should be disclosed clearly.

Shopping in the UK

- Ask for an independent gemmological report stating origin and treatment. Reputable labs include Gübelin, SSEF, GRS, and AGL; the Gem-A (Gemmological Association of Great Britain) can also help point you toward testing services. In the UK, hallmarking applies to the metal, not the stone, so it tells you nothing about treatment.
- Under UK consumer law, sellers must describe goods accurately, and gem trade guidance expects treatments to be disclosed. If a listing says “natural sapphire” and nothing else, ask directly: “Is it heated, and can you put that in writing on the invoice?”
- If a price looks too good for an “unheated” stone, treat it with suspicion. Without a lab report, “unheated” is just a claim.

Is unheated worth paying for?

If you’re buying for investment or long-term resale value, or you want a collector-grade stone, the premium is usually justified (with a certificate). If you’re buying for wearing and enjoying, a well-cut, well-coloured heated sapphire is stable, durable, and looks the same to the eye, so it can be much better value.
