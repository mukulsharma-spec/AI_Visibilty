Jewellery ideas

Birthstone pendant or necklace: her own, or her children’s stones clustered together (a “family” pendant). It’s sentimental and works at most budgets.
Stud earrings: sapphire, ruby, emerald or amethyst studs suit nearly everyone and don’t need sizing.
Gemstone ring: a stacking ring or a three-stone ring. You’ll need her size, so check a ring she already wears on that finger.
Tennis-style or delicate bracelet: small gemstones set in a line. It’s easy to wear daily and easy to size.
Drop earrings or a pendant in her favourite colour: moonstone, garnet, aquamarine and peridot are lovely and often more affordable than the “big three”.

Beyond classic jewellery

Gemstone pen or keepsake box
Polished gemstone worry stone or crystal display piece: lower cost, if she likes the look.
Vintage or antique pieces: Edwardian and Victorian garnet, amethyst and pearl pieces can be great value and have real character.

UK-specific tips

Hallmarking: gold, silver and platinum items above a small weight threshold must be hallmarked in the UK, so check for the mark. It confirms the metal purity, not the gemstone.
Natural vs lab-grown vs “simulated”: a gemstone can be real but lab-grown. If natural matters to you, ask the seller to confirm in writing.
Treatments: many natural stones (emeralds, rubies, sapphires) are routinely treated. That’s normal, but a good seller will disclose it.
Metal and plating: some fashion-fine brands use real gemstones set in gold-plated silver, which keeps costs down but wears over time. Solid gold costs more but lasts.
Returns: buying online gives you a 14-day cancellation right in most cases. Personalised or made-to-order items are usually exempt, so check the policy first.
Where to look: independent makers on Etsy UK, Hatton Garden jewellers in London for better value on loose stones and custom work, and high street names like Goldsmiths, Beaverbrooks or Ernest Jones. Brands like Monica Vinader and Astley Clarke are popular for gemstone jewellery.
