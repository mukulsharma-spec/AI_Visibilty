A few things to check before you buy:

Call it “lab-grown” or “lab-created”. Avoid vague terms like “simulated”. Simulants such as green glass or cubic zirconia aren’t emeralds at all.
Metal and hallmarking: Solid gold or platinum should be hallmarked. This matters more than the gemstone for how well the ring lasts.
Setting: Emeralds (lab or natural) are more brittle than diamonds, so a bezel or a protective setting suits an everyday ring better than a high, exposed claw.
Certification: GIA or other lab reports are less common for coloured lab stones, but some sellers offer them. They’re worth having if you’re spending over £1,500 or so.
Returns: Look for a 30-day return policy, since colour can look different in person.
