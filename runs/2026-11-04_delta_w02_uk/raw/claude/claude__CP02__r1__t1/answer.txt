What’s the difference?

Chemically and physically they’re the same: both are corundum (aluminium oxide) with trace elements giving the colour, and both score 9 on the Mohs scale. The only real difference is origin. Natural sapphires formed over millions of years in the earth, while lab-grown ones are made in weeks or months using flame fusion, flux, or hydrothermal methods.

Lab-grown stones are usually cleaner and more consistent in colour, because they’re grown without the inclusions that natural stones form with. Natural stones typically show tiny inclusions (like fine “silk” needles or crystals) and growth patterns. A good lab can tell them apart, but you can’t reliably do it by eye.

Price and value

Lab-grown sapphires are typically a small fraction of the price of a natural stone of similar size and appearance, often 5 to 10 times cheaper or more, and the gap widens at higher quality.
Natural sapphires with fine colour, good clarity, and no treatment can hold or gain value over time. Lab-grown stones generally have little to no resale value, and prices have been trending down.
Most natural sapphires on the market are heat-treated to improve colour. This is accepted in the trade but should be disclosed. Untreated natural stones command a big premium.

Which should you buy?

Choose lab-grown if you want the most beauty per pound, a larger or flawless-looking stone, or you care about avoiding mining’s environmental and ethical concerns. Just don’t buy it expecting it to hold value.
Choose natural if rarity, provenance, and long-term value matter to you, or the piece is an heirloom or investment. Ask for origin (e.g. Sri Lanka, Madagascar, Kashmir, Burma) and treatment details.

UK buying tips

Insist on clear written disclosure of “natural” or “laboratory-grown/synthetic” and of any treatments on the invoice. Misdescription can breach UK consumer protection law, and you’d have grounds for a refund.
Ask for an independent lab report for anything significant, such as from Gem-A, SSEF, GRS, or Gübelin. Reports from the retailer itself carry less weight.
Buy from reputable sellers with a clear returns policy. In Hatton Garden, look for members of the National Association of Jewellers or Gem-A–qualified gemmologists.
Remember hallmarking applies to the metal, not the stone. Gold and platinum over a minimum weight must be hallmarked by a UK assay office, but that says nothing about the sapphire.
Get it insured and valued. Insurers and valuers treat natural and lab-grown very differently.
