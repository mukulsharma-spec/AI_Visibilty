Holts Gems has bezel sapphire rings, such as a 0.62ct oval bi-colour sapphire bezel ring, with a UK size guide on the page.

What to look for, since she’s on her hands all day

A bezel, ideally flush or low. It’s a good choice here because protective settings like bezels or lower-profile designs are recommended for everyday wear, and high-set designs are best avoided.

A smaller, rounder stone. Tall or large stones sit higher on the finger even in a bezel, so a stone around 3 to 6mm will feel flatter. A smooth, polished finish with a rounded inner band. This reduces snagging, which matters when she’s constantly putting on and removing gloves. Sapphire is a good stone for this. It’s very hard and has no cleavage, so it handles knocks well.

One practical point: many NHS trusts and hospitals restrict hand jewellery in clinical areas, usually to a plain band with no stones, because of infection control. Check her workplace’s uniform policy. She may end up wearing the ring off shift or on a chain while working, which is worth knowing before you spend a lot.
