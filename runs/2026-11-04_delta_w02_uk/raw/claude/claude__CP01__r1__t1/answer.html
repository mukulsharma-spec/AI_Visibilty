Neither is objectively “better.” It depends on what you value, but here’s how they compare.

Durability: Diamond is a 10 on the Mohs scale, sapphire a 9. Both are tough enough for daily wear, but diamond resists scratching and chipping slightly better. A sapphire will eventually show some micro-abrasion over decades, though it’s still one of the best choices after diamond.

Appearance: Diamonds are prized for brilliance and sparkle, with a colourless, icy look. Sapphires offer depth of colour (classic blue, but also pink, yellow, green, and more) and a softer, richer glow. Sapphires tend to look more distinctive, while diamonds are the traditional choice.

Price: For the same size and comparable quality, sapphires are typically considerably cheaper per carat, so you can often get a larger, higher-quality stone for the budget. Prices vary widely with colour, clarity, origin, and treatment, so it’s worth comparing specific stones rather than relying on averages.

Treatments and ethics: Most sapphires are heat-treated, which is standard and accepted but should be disclosed. Untreated stones command a premium. For both stones, ask about provenance. Diamonds can be bought with traceability, or lab-grown, which is much cheaper than mined. Sapphires can come from Sri Lanka, Madagascar, Australia, Montana, and elsewhere, and ethical sourcing varies by seller.

Symbolism: Diamonds are the conventional engagement stone. Sapphires have royal associations in the UK (Princess Diana’s ring, now worn by the Princess of Wales), which many people love.

UK buying tips:

Ask for an independent grading or gemmological report: GIA, IGI, or HRD for diamonds; GIA, SSEF, or Gübelin for higher-value sapphires.
Check the jeweller is a member of the National Association of Jewellers (NAJ) or similar, and look for hallmarked metal (platinum, 18ct gold, etc.).
Hatton Garden in London is a well-known area for comparing stones and prices in person.
A setting that protects the stone, such as a bezel or low-set claws, helps either gem last.

Rough rule of thumb: Choose diamond if you want tradition, maximum durability, and classic sparkle. Choose sapphire if you want colour, individuality, and more size or quality for your money.
