For a ring under about £1,100, you could:

  • Email the seller before buying and ask whether the ruby comes with a gemstone report (from a lab such as GIA, SSEF, Gübelin, IGI or GRS) and what treatment it has had. Reputable sellers will answer plainly.
  • Ask for a written treatment disclosure if there’s no lab report. Heat treatment is normal for rubies, but you want it stated, and “glass-filled” or “composite” should be ruled out.
  • Expect certificates to be rare at this price. A lab report costs money, so they mostly come with larger or higher-value stones. For a small ruby in 9ct or 18ct gold, a clear “natural, heat-treated” statement plus a good returns policy is a reasonable standard.
  • Check the hallmark. UK law requires gold items over 1g to carry a hallmark from an assay office. That verifies the metal, not the stone.
