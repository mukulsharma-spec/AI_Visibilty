For an emerald specifically, I’d lean Angara on value. Brilliant Earth is the stronger pick if you want a more polished service experience and don’t mind paying more. I couldn’t find like-for-like UK prices for the same stone, so this is based on what I could see of each range, and you should compare actual quotes.

Why Angara tends to win on value: Colour gemstones are its core business, and it has a big emerald range. In the UK it offers free express shipping, free return shipping within the UK and free 60-day returns. As one price point, a lab-grown 10x8mm oval emerald solitaire was listed at $1,907, down from $2,119. Brilliant Earth’s emerald page, by comparison, lists a 10x8mm lab-grown emerald at $2,290, a 6.9x4.9mm natural emerald at $2,920, and an 8.2x7mm Colombian emerald at $6,600. That is a US page and I can’t tell whether those prices include a setting, so treat it as a rough indication only.

Where Brilliant Earth is stronger: Emeralds are a smaller part of its business, since it’s mainly known for diamonds. It does offer a free lifetime warranty, and for UK orders it collects the relevant duties itself, so there are no surprise import charges.

What matters more than the retailer:

Treatment: Both sell oiled emeralds, which is standard. Angara’s product pages disclose oiling as the enhancement, and Brilliant Earth says its emeralds are treated by oil infusion. Ask about the level of treatment, because heavy oiling is a red flag.

Grading: Angara’s AA to AAAA grades are its own scale, not an independent standard. For a natural stone costing more than about £1,500, ask for a report from an independent lab such as GIA, SSEF or Gübelin. I could see GIA certificates on some of Angara’s lab-grown rings, but check each ring. Lab-grown vs natural: A lab-grown emerald usually gives you a cleaner, more vivid stone at a much lower price, and both retailers sell them. Natural stones with good clarity get expensive quickly. Durability: Emeralds chip easily. A ring that’s too delicate is a real risk, and one Brilliant Earth reviewer said theirs was so delicate it ended up unwearable. Choose a bezel or halo setting with protective prongs, and avoid thin, exposed claws.

I’d shortlist two or three comparable stones from each (same size, origin and treatment) and ask both for photos or video in natural light, since emerald colour looks very different on screen. If you tell me your budget and whether you want natural or lab-grown, I can narrow it down.
