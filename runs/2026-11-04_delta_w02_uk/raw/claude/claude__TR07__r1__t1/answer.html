There are several UK options, but “ethically sourced” is largely self-declared for coloured gemstones, so it’s worth asking questions whatever you buy.

Why it needs care

Around 80% of coloured gemstones come from small-scale mines involving roughly 30 million miners, which makes formalising the industry very hard. There’s no equivalent of the Kimberley Process to certify coloured gems from mine to market. So the strongest signal is a short, documented chain from mine to jeweller, rather than a label. Where to look

Nineteen48 and Rubyfair (loose stones): two of the UK’s leading suppliers of traceable coloured gemstones, now merged. Nineteen48 specialises in fully traceable Sri Lankan stones such as sapphires, spinels, garnets and moonstones, while Rubyfair offers tanzanite, ruby and sapphire. Nineteen48, Fair Trade Gemstones and Capricorn Gems have also run a UK fair for gems with traceable sources in Sri Lanka, Australia, Malawi, Tanzania and the USA. [benchpeg] [benchpeg]

Holts Gems (Hatton Garden): publishes a guide to its ethical sourcing and traceability. [holtsgems]

London DE (bespoke jewellery and emeralds): says its emeralds are ethically sourced and fairly traded from mine to market, and notes that fewer hands means more traceability. [jewelleryfocus]

Just D: states its diamonds and gemstones are traced from their mine of origin to the finished piece. [justind]

Ingle & Rhode (rings): uses traceable Canadian diamonds, Fairtrade gold or recycled platinum, and “ethically sourced” sapphires, emeralds and rubies. [weddingplanner]

Pragnell: says it applies the same ethical principles to coloured gemstones, relying on gemmologist assessment and supplier relationships. That’s a reputable house, but less specific about traceability than the smaller specialists. [pragnell]

How to vet any seller

Ask for the specific mine or region and the supply chain. “Ethically sourced” with no origin detail is a weak claim. Be sceptical of vague wording.
Prefer fewer intermediaries. Direct-from-cutter or direct-from-mine relationships are the best indicator.
Check for third-party frameworks. The Responsible Jewellery Council certification and the Coloured Gemstones Working Group’s platform are two of the main ones. Neither is perfect, but they show a seller is engaging with standards. [seekd]
Expect limited choice. Even ethical designers note that fully traceable options are still limited, and fashionable stones are often impossible to trace. Sri Lankan sapphires and spinels, Australian stones and some African origins tend to be easiest. If you’re set on a specific stone, such as a Burmese ruby, traceability will be much harder. [shopify] [lebrusanstudio]
Consider antique or recycled stones. Reusing existing gems sidesteps new mining entirely, and many Hatton Garden and antique dealers sell them.
