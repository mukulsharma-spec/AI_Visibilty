There are a few good routes in the UK, depending on how closely you want the band to match.

High street and large retailers (easy to try on in person)

Goldsmiths, Mappin & Webb, Ernest Jones, Beaverbrooks, Fraser Hart: wide ranges of plain, shaped and diamond-set bands, and staff will let you try bands against your actual ring. Hatton Garden, London: dozens of independent jewellers on one street, many of whom make bands to order at a good price.

Online and made-to-order

Taylor & Hart, Lily Arkwright, Angelic Diamonds: UK makers with customisable bands, including curved or notched designs and sapphire or diamond accents. Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth: both ship to the UK, but check import duty and sizing and returns policies first.

Bespoke or vintage

If your ring has an unusual setting, a local independent jeweller can make a band that fits it exactly. This is often the best way to get a perfect match. For an antique or vintage sapphire ring, look at dealers like Hancocks or Berganza, or auction houses and antique jewellery fairs.

Tips for a good match

Shape matters more than colour. Many sapphire rings have a raised setting or an oval or cushion stone that stops a straight band sitting flush. A curved, shaped or “wishbone” band solves this. Match the metal and carat. White gold, yellow gold and platinum all age differently, so match the metal and purity (for example, 9ct, 18ct or platinum) to your ring. Bring the ring with you if you can, or buy the band from the same jeweller as the ring. Many will offer a matching band or a discount on one. Check for a UK hallmark on anything you buy.
