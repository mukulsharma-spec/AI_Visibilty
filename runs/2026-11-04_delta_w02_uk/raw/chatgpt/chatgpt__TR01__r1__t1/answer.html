When shopping for natural gemstones online in the UK, the most important thing is to establish whether the stone is genuinely natural, whether it has been treated, and whether the seller can prove what they're claiming.

A beautiful gemstone photograph isn't proof of authenticity. Even reputable-looking jewellery websites can use vague descriptions that make synthetic or treated stones sound more valuable than they are.

Here's how I'd check an online jeweller before spending money.

1. Look for the right terminology

Pay close attention to the words used in the product description.

Natural sapphire = Mined from the earth

Lab-grown sapphire = Man-made; same basic chemical composition

Synthetic ruby = Man-made, not natural

Simulated emerald = Another material made to look like emerald

Natural, heated ruby = Natural gemstone that has undergone heat treatment

Natural, untreated emerald = Natural stone with no treatment claimed

Important: A treated gemstone can still be 100% natural. Heating, oiling, irradiation and diffusion are different treatments, and they can significantly affect value.

Be particularly cautious of descriptions such as: Genuine gemstone; Real gemstone; Natural-looking stone; Precious gemstone; AAA quality

These terms alone don't establish that a stone is natural.

2. Ask for an independent gemstone certificate

One of the strongest checks

For a more expensive gemstone, ask whether the seller can provide an independent gemmological laboratory report.

Some recognised laboratories include:

GIA

Gemological Institute of America. Well-known for gemstone identification and treatment reports.

Official website

Gübelin Gem Lab

Highly regarded independent gemmological laboratory, particularly for coloured gemstones.

Official website

SSEF

Specialises in the testing and identification of precious gemstones.

Official website

Gem-A

UK-based gemmological organisation. Useful for finding qualified gemmological expertise.

Official website

A proper report should identify the gemstone and, where applicable, disclose detectable treatments and whether the stone is natural or laboratory-grown.

For expensive purchases, check the report number directly with the issuing laboratory. A certificate supplied by the seller isn't automatically independent verification.

3. Check whether the seller is transparent about each stone

A trustworthy online jeweller should ideally provide:

Individual photographs: Not just generic catalogue images.

Carat weight: The actual weight of the gemstone.

Measurements: For example, 7 × 5 mm.

Treatment status: Heated, unheated, oiled, untreated, etc.

Gemstone identity: For example, natural emerald rather than simply green gemstone.

Origin: If claimed, explain whether it is laboratory-confirmed or seller-supplied.

Return policy: A reasonable opportunity to return the item if it isn't as described.

The UK's National Association of Jewellers has published guidance on gemstone descriptions, emphasising accurate disclosure of gemstone characteristics and treatments. [National Association of Jewellers +1 — citation chip, no href]

4. Watch out for these red flags

I'd be particularly careful if a jeweller:

Claims to sell untreated rubies, emeralds or sapphires at unusually low prices.

Says every gemstone is natural but offers no meaningful identification information.

Uses the word "certified" without naming the laboratory.

Provides its own certificate but no way to verify the stone independently.

Claims a gemstone comes from Kashmir, Burma, Colombia or another famous origin without supporting evidence.

Refuses to answer questions about treatments.

Has no clear UK business address or returns information.

One important distinction: a gemstone certificate does not automatically prove its geographical origin, rarity or market value. These are separate claims requiring separate evidence.

5. Check UK jewellery protections

There are two things worth checking:

Precious metal hallmarking

If you're buying gold, silver, platinum or palladium jewellery, check whether it should carry a UK hallmark. There are legal exemptions for certain items, including those below specified weight thresholds.

The UK government explains how to check hallmarks and seller obligations.

UK government hallmarking guidance . [GOV.UK — citation chip, no href]

Online shopping returns

For most online purchases from UK traders, you generally have 14 days after receiving the item to notify the seller that you want to cancel, followed by another 14 days to return it. Exceptions apply, including certain personalised or bespoke items.

You also have consumer rights if an item is not as described.

6. A message you can send to any online jeweller

Before ordering, I'd send this:

Hello,

I'm interested in purchasing one of your gemstones and would appreciate clarification on a few things before ordering.

Is the gemstone 100% natural and earth-mined, rather than laboratory-grown, synthetic or imitation?

Has it undergone any treatments, such as heating, oiling, irradiation, dyeing or diffusion?

Do you provide an independent gemmological laboratory certificate, such as GIA, SSEF or Gübelin?

Does the certificate correspond to the exact gemstone being sold?

Can you confirm the gemstone's carat weight, dimensions and geographical origin, if known?

Are the photographs of the actual gemstone?

Can I return the gemstone if independent testing establishes that it isn't as described?

I'm specifically looking for genuine natural gemstones, with transparent disclosure of any treatments.

Thank you.

My recommendation

I'd use three standards when choosing a UK online jeweller:

Natural gemstone identification: Independent evidence for expensive stones.

Treatment transparency: Clear disclosure of whether the gemstone is heated, oiled, enhanced or untreated.

Written assurance: The seller puts its claims on the invoice and offers a sensible return policy.
