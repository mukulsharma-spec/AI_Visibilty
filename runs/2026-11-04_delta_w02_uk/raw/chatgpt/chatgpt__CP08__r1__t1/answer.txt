What do gemstone quality grades like AA, AAA and AAAA mean? I'm shopping in the UK.
When shopping for gemstones in the UK, you'll often see grades such as AA, AAA and AAAA. Generally, the more As, the higher the claimed quality. However, there's an important catch: these grades are not part of a universally recognised gemstone grading system.
Unlike diamond grading, coloured gemstones don't have one internationally standardised AA–AAAA scale.
The Gemological Institute of America (GIA) explicitly confirms this for gemstones such as amethyst, spinel and aquamarine.

2. What actually determines gemstone quality?
I would pay more attention to these five factors than the number of As.
1. Colour
Look for attractive, even colour with good saturation. Avoid stones that look excessively grey, brown or washed out, unless that appearance is characteristic of the variety.
2. Clarity
Check for inclusions, cracks and cloudy areas. Remember that some inclusions are completely normal in natural gemstones, particularly emeralds.
3. Cut
A well-cut gemstone should reflect light attractively, with balanced facets and no obvious dull or see-through patches.
4. Transparency
Check whether the gemstone has good transparency for its particular variety. Some gems are naturally opaque or translucent.
5. Treatments and authenticity
Find out whether the stone is natural, laboratory-grown, heated, irradiated, dyed, fracture-filled or otherwise treated.
3. What should you look for when buying in the UK?
I'd recommend the following precautions:
Ask for a gemmological report: Particularly for expensive rubies, sapphires, emeralds and other valuable stones.
Check the issuing laboratory: A grading certificate is only as useful as the laboratory and the information it provides.
Ask whether the gemstone is natural or lab-grown.
Get treatment information in writing: Treatments can substantially affect value.
Ask what the seller means by AAA or AAAA: Request their actual grading criteria rather than relying on the label.
Check the returns policy: Particularly when purchasing online.
In the UK, you can look into reports from organisations such as
Gem-A and established independent gemmological laboratories.
4. My recommendation
I wouldn't automatically pay a premium for AAAA over AAA.
A beautiful, well-cut AAA gemstone with attractive colour can be a much better purchase than a poorly cut AAAA stone.
The grade should be a starting point, not the deciding factor.
