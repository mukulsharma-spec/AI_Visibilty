The honest picture from customer reviews is that buying coloured gemstones online in the UK can be very rewarding, but the biggest source of disappointment is the difference between how a gemstone looks on screen and how it looks in real life.

People generally aren't worried about whether a stone is beautiful in a photograph. They're worried about whether the stone they receive is actually the same colour, clarity, size and quality they thought they were paying for.

There are a few recurring themes worth understanding before spending money, particularly if you're buying a sapphire, emerald, ruby, tourmaline, spinel or another loose gemstone.

What customers actually complain about

1. Colour doesn't match the photograph

This is probably the most persistent complaint. Stones can look brighter, more saturated or lighter in professional photographs than they do in ordinary daylight.

One Reddit buyer showed a red spinel that arrived much darker and more purple than the seller's vivid-red photograph.

2. Inclusions and imperfections are under-disclosed

Customers sometimes find cracks, cloudy areas, surface marks or inclusions that weren't obvious in the listing.

The Curious Gem has overwhelmingly positive reviews, but some reviewers specifically mention unexpected cracks and unclear sizing descriptions.

3. Natural, treated and synthetic stones aren't always clearly distinguished

This is particularly important with emeralds, sapphires and rubies.

Buyers want to know whether a stone is natural, laboratory-grown, heat-treated, fracture-filled, dyed or otherwise enhanced. Online complaints include disputed authenticity and certificates that don't match the stones.

4. The actual size is smaller than expected

Carat weight can be misleading because different gemstones have different densities.

Customers benefit from millimetre measurements, not just carat weight. This complaint also appears in jewellery reviews, where pieces can look much larger online.

What I would insist on before buying

If I were buying a loose coloured gemstone online, I'd use this checklist:

A video of the actual stone, not a stock video or promotional clip.

Photographs in daylight, indoor light and neutral lighting.

Exact measurements in millimetres, plus carat weight.

Written confirmation of whether it is natural or laboratory-grown.

Full disclosure of treatments, including heating, filling, diffusion or dyeing where relevant.

Clear disclosure of visible inclusions, cracks and chips.

An independent laboratory report for a valuable purchase, where appropriate.

A written returns policy that lets you inspect the stone at home.

A UK business address and clear contact details.

A particularly important UK consideration

Buying from an overseas seller can make a seemingly inexpensive gemstone considerably more expensive. Import VAT, possible customs duty and courier handling charges can apply. Check the official GOV.UK import tax guidance .

My biggest recommendation

Don't choose a gemstone retailer just because it has beautiful photographs or thousands of positive reviews.

Choose one that is happy to show you the exact stone under ordinary lighting, explain its treatments and imperfections, and accept a return if it doesn't match the description.
