For coloured gemstones in the UK, I'd generally lean towards buying from an independent coloured-gemstone specialist rather than a large jewellery chain, particularly if you're buying a significant stone such as a sapphire, ruby, emerald, spinel or tourmaline.
However, there are advantages to both, and the best choice depends on whether you're prioritising gemstone quality, price, design, or peace of mind.
Specialist vs big jewellery chain
1. Independent gemstone specialist
My preferred option
Usually a much wider selection of unusual and fine-quality stones.
Better knowledge of treatments, origins, colour and clarity.
More likely to explain why two visually similar stones have very different prices.
Often more flexibility around sourcing a particular stone.
Potentially better value, particularly for unusual gemstones.
Downside: Quality and ethics vary between dealers. You need to establish their reputation.
2. Large jewellery chain
Convenient, established retail presence.
Easier to see finished jewellery in person.
Often straightforward returns, warranties and after-sales service.
Consistent retail experience.
Downside: More limited gemstone selection, less specialist expertise and potentially higher mark-ups for the overall jewellery design and branding.
What matters most when buying in the UK
I'd pay particular attention to five things, regardless of where you shop.
1. Treatment disclosure. Ask whether the stone is heated, oiled, fracture-filled, irradiated, diffusion-treated or untreated. Treatments can make a substantial difference to value. The UK's National Association of Jewellers has useful guidance on this.

2. Natural versus lab-grown. Make sure the distinction is explicitly stated. Both can be beautiful, but their prices are very different.
3. Independent laboratory report. For an expensive ruby, sapphire or emerald, I'd want a report from an independent gemmological laboratory, not just the seller's own certificate.

+1
4. Return policy. Ideally, have at least 14 days to inspect the stone, particularly when buying online. Check the actual terms, including exceptions for bespoke or commissioned jewellery.
5. Viewing conditions. Look at the gemstone in natural daylight as well as shop lighting. Colour can look dramatically different under different lighting.

UK resources worth knowing
These are useful independent organisations and resources:
National Association of Jewellers – Website . Useful consumer guidance and information about reputable jewellers.
The Gem and Pearl Laboratory – Website . Independent gemstone identification and reports.
Gemmological Certification Services (GCS) – Website . Independent gemstone testing, including treatment and origin assessments.
My recommendation
I'd actually consider using two different businesses:
Buy the gemstone from a specialist who knows coloured stones exceptionally well.
Have it independently assessed, particularly if it's an expensive purchase.
Have it made into jewellery by a good independent jeweller, if necessary.
This separates gemstone expertise from jewellery retailing.
