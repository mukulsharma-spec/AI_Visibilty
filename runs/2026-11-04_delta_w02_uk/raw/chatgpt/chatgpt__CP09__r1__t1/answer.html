is a heated sapphire worth less than an unheated one? I'm shopping in the UK.

Yes. A heated sapphire is generally worth less than an equivalent unheated sapphire, sometimes significantly less.

The reason is rarity: natural sapphires that have not undergone heat treatment are rarer and more desirable to collectors. Heating is a common industry practice used to improve a sapphire's colour and clarity.

However, heated doesn't mean poor quality or a bad purchase. A beautiful, well-cut heated sapphire can be an excellent choice.

The price difference isn't fixed. Depending on the stone, an unheated sapphire can command a premium of 20–100% or considerably more over a comparable heated stone.

What I'd recommend when shopping in the UK

Ask for an independent laboratory report. Ideally from GIA, SSEF, Gübelin or GRS.

Look for wording such as "No indications of heating" rather than relying on the seller's description.

Check that the stone is natural, not lab-grown.

Make sure there are no additional treatments, particularly beryllium diffusion or fracture filling.

Compare colour, cut and clarity before paying a premium for unheated status.

Is unheated worth paying extra for?

My view:

For an engagement ring or everyday jewellery: I'd happily buy a heated sapphire if it had beautiful colour and a fair price.

For rarity and long-term collectability: I'd favour an unheated sapphire with a reputable laboratory report.

For investment: I'd be cautious about either. Sapphires aren't guaranteed to appreciate or resell easily.
