Why I'd favour a jeweller
There are three things that matter enormously when buying gemstones:
Natural vs synthetic: A natural sapphire, ruby or emerald is different from a lab-created equivalent. Both can be beautiful, but their prices differ significantly.
Treatments: Heat treatment, oiling, dyeing and other enhancements can affect value. These should be disclosed.
Certification: For expensive purchases, you want an independent gemological report, not simply a seller's certificate saying the stone is genuine.
A beautiful-looking gemstone isn't necessarily a valuable or natural gemstone.
What to check when buying in the UK
I'd use these as my checklist:
Hallmark: If buying gold or silver, check the appropriate UK hallmark requirements. GOV.UK explains how to verify them.
GOV.UK
Natural or lab-grown: Make sure the listing explicitly states which.
Treatments: Ask whether the stone is heated, dyed, oiled, irradiated or otherwise enhanced.
Independent report: For expensive rubies, emeralds and sapphires, look for reports from recognised gemological laboratories.

When is Amazon okay?
I'd happily consider Amazon for:
Affordable birthstone jewellery.
Fashion jewellery with inexpensive stones.
Silver-plated or gold-plated accessories.
Jewellery where you're primarily buying for the design rather than gemstone value.
I'd avoid Amazon for expensive natural emeralds, rubies, sapphires and investment-oriented gemstone purchases.
