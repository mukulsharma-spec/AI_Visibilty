Sapphire vs diamond for an engagement ring: which is better? I'm shopping in the UK.
For an engagement ring, diamond and sapphire are both excellent choices, but they offer quite different things. The biggest differences are durability, appearance, price and long-term practicality.
My initial recommendation: if you want a traditional, colourless engagement ring that is extremely durable, choose a diamond. If you want something distinctive, colourful and potentially better value, choose a sapphire.

Important: Sapphire is very durable for everyday wear, but diamonds are harder. Both can chip if struck at the wrong angle.
2. Which lasts longer?
Diamond advantage
Diamonds score 10 on the Mohs hardness scale, compared with 9 for sapphires.
However, hardness isn't the same as overall toughness. Both stones are suitable for engagement rings worn daily.
Diamond: excellent resistance to scratching, but can chip or fracture.
Sapphire: excellent scratch resistance and good toughness, although it can also chip.
A sapphire engagement ring can comfortably last generations with sensible care.
3. Which looks more beautiful?
This is largely a matter of personal taste.
Sapphire
Rich colour, depth and a more understated sparkle. Particularly beautiful in oval and cushion cuts.
Diamond
More intense white sparkle, flashes of rainbow colour and a classic engagement-ring appearance.
One thing worth knowing: a white sapphire isn't necessarily a cheaper-looking equivalent to a diamond. It generally has less brilliance and fire, so the two don't look identical.
4. Which is better value in the UK?
Sapphire advantage for natural stones
Generally, a natural sapphire costs less than a natural diamond of comparable visual size and quality. But there are exceptions: rare, untreated, top-quality sapphires can be extremely expensive.
There is another option worth considering: lab-grown diamonds.

5. What should you check before buying in the UK?
This is particularly important with sapphires because their quality and treatments can vary considerably.
If buying a sapphire:
Check whether it's natural or lab-created.
Ask whether it's heated or unheated. Heat treatment is common and generally accepted, but must be disclosed.
Ask for an independent gem laboratory report for a valuable stone, particularly one over 1ct.
Look at the stone in daylight as well as shop lighting.
Check the colour isn't so dark that it appears almost black indoors.
If buying a diamond:
Request a GIA or IGI grading report.
Compare cut, colour, clarity and carat weight (the 4Cs).
Establish whether it's natural or lab-grown.
Don't pay a premium for a high clarity grade if a lower grade looks identical to the naked eye.
