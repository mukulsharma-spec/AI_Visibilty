Yes — Etsy can be a good place to buy a genuine sapphire ring in the UK, but I'd be quite selective about the seller.
There are some lovely independent jewellers on Etsy, including sellers offering natural sapphires, vintage rings and handmade settings. However, Etsy is a marketplace, not a guarantee of gemstone authenticity.
The biggest thing to understand is that a real sapphire isn't necessarily a natural, untreated sapphire.
For example:
Natural sapphire: Mined from the earth.
Heat-treated natural sapphire: Genuine natural sapphire that has been heated to improve its colour or clarity. Very common and generally accepted.
Lab-created sapphire: Chemically a real sapphire, but grown in a laboratory rather than mined.
Simulated sapphire: A different material made to look like sapphire.
All four can be advertised in ways that confuse buyers.
What I'd check before buying on Etsy
I'd insist on these five things:
Written confirmation it's natural: Ask whether the sapphire is earth-mined, not lab-grown.
Treatment disclosure: Is it heated, unheated, diffusion-treated or fracture-filled?
Independent certificate: Ideally a report from GIA, GRS, SSEF or another reputable gemmological laboratory. Ask for the actual report number so you can verify it.
Clear metal specification: 9ct, 14ct or 18ct solid gold, or 925 sterling silver. Be careful with gold-plated jewellery.
A proper returns policy: Etsy sellers set their own policies, and not every item is returnable.

My advice for buying in the UK
I'd be particularly cautious about:
Very large, vivid blue sapphires at surprisingly low prices.
Listings using the word "certified" without naming the laboratory.
Sellers who won't answer questions about treatments.
Shops with lots of generic gemstone listings and very little information about the actual stones.
Etsy does have some interesting independent jewellers, but I wouldn't spend serious money based on reviews and pretty photographs alone.
