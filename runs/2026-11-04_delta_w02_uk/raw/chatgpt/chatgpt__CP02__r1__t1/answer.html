Natural vs lab-grown sapphire: what's the difference and which should I buy? I'm shopping in the UK.

If you're buying a sapphire in the UK, the biggest difference is origin, rarity and resale value – not appearance or durability.

A lab-grown sapphire is a real sapphire, not an imitation. It has essentially the same chemical composition, crystal structure, hardness and optical properties as a natural sapphire.

My general recommendation: choose lab-grown if you want the most beautiful stone for your money; choose natural if rarity, geological origin and long-term collectability matter to you.

2. The important detail: natural doesn't automatically mean better

There are three categories worth understanding:

Natural, heated: Mined from the earth and heat-treated to improve colour or clarity. This is perfectly legitimate when disclosed.

Natural, unheated: Mined and has no indication of heat treatment. Generally rarer and more expensive.

Lab-grown: Created in a laboratory. Can have excellent colour and clarity at a fraction of the cost.

I wouldn't automatically pay a large premium for an unheated sapphire. The actual colour, cut and beauty of the stone matter enormously.

4. What to look for when buying

9

These are the qualities I'd prioritise:

Colour: Look for rich, vivid colour rather than a stone so dark it looks black indoors.

Cut: A well-cut sapphire should have attractive colour distribution without obvious dark or washed-out areas.

Clarity: Minor inclusions are normal in natural sapphires. Avoid prominent cracks or cloudy patches.

Treatment: Establish whether it's heated, unheated or otherwise treated.

Certification: For a significant natural sapphire purchase, request an independent gemmological report from a recognised laboratory such as GIA, SSEF or Gübelin.

A particularly important UK buying tip: get the seller to state in writing whether the sapphire is natural or laboratory-grown. Don't rely on descriptions such as "genuine sapphire" or "created sapphire" without clarification.

5. My buying recommendation

Choose lab-grown if:

You want a large, beautiful blue stone.

You want excellent colour without spending thousands.

You're buying primarily for wearing and enjoyment.

Choose natural if:

You love the idea of a gemstone formed naturally over millions of years.

You value rarity and provenance.

You're comfortable paying considerably more for a fine-quality stone.

I would not buy either expecting it to be an investment. Even natural sapphires can be difficult to resell at anything close to their original retail price.
