An important distinction: lab-created emeralds are not green glass or imitation stones. They are genuine emeralds grown in controlled laboratory conditions.

They have essentially the same chemical and physical properties as mined emeralds. They can also be quite durable, although emeralds are more prone to chipping than diamonds, so protective settings are sensible.

One thing to know: Lab-created emeralds generally have much lower resale value. Buy one because you love wearing it, rather than as an investment.

3. What I'd look for when buying

A few things make a big difference:

Colour: Look for a rich, saturated green rather than something too pale or unnaturally neon.

Cut: Oval, cushion and emerald-cut stones are particularly attractive.

Metal: Yellow gold looks gorgeous with emerald; white gold gives a cooler, more contemporary appearance.

Stone disclosure: Make sure it explicitly says lab-created emerald, not green cubic zirconia, green sapphire or glass.

Certificate: Ask for documentation identifying the gemstone and its laboratory-grown origin.

Setting: For a ring, choose a setting that protects the corners and edges.

One useful distinction: some sellers use the term created emerald and others use lab-grown emerald. Both can refer to genuine laboratory-grown emerald, but always check the actual gemstone description.
