A ruby engagement ring is a beautiful alternative to a diamond. ❤️ Rubies have a rich, romantic red colour, are exceptionally durable, and have a hardness of 9 on the Mohs scale (just below diamond at 10), making them suitable for everyday wear.

For buying in the UK, I'd concentrate on jewellers offering natural, untreated or responsibly disclosed rubies, independent gemstone certification, and good aftercare.

I'd also look beyond traditional high-street engagement rings. Some of the most beautiful ruby rings come from specialist coloured-gemstone jewellers.

What I'd look for in the ruby itself

There are a few important things I'd insist on before spending serious money:

Natural versus lab-grown: Decide which you want. Both are real rubies, but natural stones are generally more expensive.

Treatment: Heat-treated rubies are perfectly legitimate and common. Avoid lead-glass-filled or composite rubies for an engagement ring.

Certification: For a significant natural ruby, ask for an independent report from GIA, SSEF, Gübelin or GRS.

Colour: Look for a vivid, rich red rather than a stone that looks too pink, brown or dark.

Setting: Make sure the ruby is protected by a secure setting, especially if it's an everyday ring.

The GIA's ruby buying guide is a useful independent reference.

One thing worth knowing: don't feel you have to pay a huge premium for a ruby advertised as "pigeon blood". The actual colour and beauty of the stone matter more than a marketing description.

How much should you budget?

As a rough starting point for UK shopping:

£500–£1,000: Smaller natural ruby or lab-grown ruby

£1,000–£2,000: Attractive natural ruby in a gold setting

£2,000–£3,500: Good bespoke options, potentially a 1ct natural ruby

£3,500–£6,000: Higher-quality ruby, more elaborate setting

£6,000+: Premium natural stones and more exceptional craftsmanship

These are shopping guides rather than guaranteed quality brackets. Ruby prices vary enormously with colour, treatment and origin.
