How to make absolutely sure it's real

This is particularly important because synthetic alexandrite can look almost identical to natural alexandrite.

Before paying, insist on:

A GIA, GRS, SSEF or Gübelin laboratory report. It should identify the stone as natural chrysoberyl, variety alexandrite.

A clear statement that it is not laboratory-grown.

A report matching the actual stone by weight, measurements and identifying details.

A genuine colour-change demonstration. Natural alexandrite typically appears green or bluish-green in daylight and reddish-purple under incandescent lighting.

A written return policy allowing you to have the stone independently checked.

An appraisal or a jeweller's own certificate is not a substitute for an independent gemological report.

What sort of budget should you expect?

Natural alexandrite can be considerably more expensive than lab-grown material.

Be especially suspicious of large, supposedly natural alexandrite rings selling for a few hundred pounds.
