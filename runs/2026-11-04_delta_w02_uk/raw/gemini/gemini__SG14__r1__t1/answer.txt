Shopping for a nurse who works extensively with her hands requires a very specific type of ring. A full bezel set (where a continuous rim of metal wraps completely around the stone) combined with a low profile is the gold standard. It eliminates high-profile prongs that snag on medical gloves, prevents crevices from trapping hand sanitizer or bacteria, and protects the sapphire against accidental knocks against equipment.

Sapphire is an excellent choice because it’s a 9 on the Mohs hardness scale (second only to diamond), making it tough enough to handle frequent hand-washing and daily wear.

Tips for Buying a Nurse-Friendly Sapphire Ring:

Choose the Right Metal: Opt for Platinum or 14ct/18ct Gold. Avoid 9ct gold if she washes her hands constantly, as lower-carat alloys can sometimes react more noticeably to harsh clinical soaps and sanitizers over many years, whereas platinum and 18ct hold up robustly.

Opt for a Full Bezel Over a Half-Bezel: A full bezel wraps 360 degrees around the stone. This means absolute zero corners or edges for medical gloves to tear on.

Check the Underside: Ensure the basket underneath the stone isn't hollowed out in a way that traps moisture and soap, but rather smoothly polished so it's easy to clean under running water.
