Short answer: Tanzanite is a stunning choice for an engagement ring, but it comes with significant caveats regarding durability.

Because an engagement ring is meant to be worn every single day for decades, tanzanite requires a lot more care and mindfulness than traditional stones like diamonds or sapphires.

1. The Durability Challenge (The Mohs Scale & Cleavage)

Hardness (6.5 to 7): Tanzanite is moderately hard, sitting around the same level as quartz (amethyst/citrine). While it resists everyday dust and light scratching, over years of daily wear, it can pick up micro-scratches and lose its crisp polish if it rubs against harder materials or dust particles (which often contain quartz).

Cleavage and Brittleness: This is the bigger risk. Tanzanite has a structural property called cleavage, meaning it has planes in its crystal lattice where it can split or crack if hit at the wrong angle. A sharp knock against a door frame, a gym weight, or a kitchen counter can chip or even fracture the stone.

2. How to Make It Work (If You Still Want One)

If you or your partner have fallen in love with tanzanite’s mesmerizing violet-blue depth and want to proceed, you can mitigate the risks significantly with the right design choices:

Choose a Protective Setting: Never choose a high, delicate, four-prong solitaire setting that leaves the sides and edges (girdle) of the stone completely exposed. Instead, opt for a bezel setting (where a rim of metal wraps securely around the stone's edges) or a secure halo setting where surrounding diamonds take the brunt of accidental side impacts.

Consider Side Stones: A three-stone ring layout can help buffer the central tanzanite from lateral knocks.

Lifestyle Habits: It must be treated as "occasional" fine jewellery rather than indestructible armor. It should be taken off before gardening, heavy cleaning, DIY, gym workouts, sleeping, and swimming.

3. Shopping for Tanzanite in the UK

If you decide to buy in the UK, keep these practical points in mind:

Where to Look: You can find tanzanite easily on the UK high street (through major chains like The Diamond Store or Ernest Jones, as well as televised jewellery channels like TJC) and via independent Hatton Garden (London) jewellers who can custom-set a loose stone for you.

Grading Caveats: Unlike diamonds, tanzanite doesn't have an internationally standardized grading body (like the GIA's cut/color/clarity charts for diamonds). Terms like "AAA" or "AAAA" are self-applied by individual vendors and wholesalers, so always trust your eyes and buy from a reputable UK jeweler who offers a clear certificate of authenticity.

The "Single Source" Factor: Tanzanite is famously mined in only one place on Earth—near Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. Because supply is finite, high-grade stones hold their value well as a collector's gemstone.

Alternative Options

If you want that exact deep blue/purple color with much higher durability for daily wear, look into blue sapphires (Mohs 9) or heat-treated blueish-purple spinels.

If you love tanzanite specifically for its unique story, rarity, and color-shifting properties (pleochroism), just be prepared to commit to a protective bezel setting and mindful everyday wear.
