Not knowing her ring size is completely normal, and UK jewelers are very used to working around it. Depending on how you want to handle the surprise, you have a few great routes to choose from:

1. Major High Street Names with "Proposal Ring" Schemes

Several well-known UK jewelers offer formal placeholder or proposal ring services. They sell an affordable stand-in ring (often silver or cubic zirconia) for you to propose with. Once she says yes, you return to the store together to get her exact size and pick out the "forever" ring.

Beaverbrooks: They offer a dedicated range of silver and cubic zirconia proposal rings. If you buy your actual engagement ring with them afterward, they often credit the cost of the proposal ring back or deduct it.

Independent Specialists (e.g., The Cornish Diamond Co. / Proposal Ring UK): Brands like The Cornish Diamond Co. feature "The Yes Ring," where you buy a temporary ring, propose, and then roll the entire cost over into the main engagement ring. Dedicated online sites like Proposalring.co.uk also sell adjustable stand-in rings specifically built to fit sizes I through S.

2. Online Retailers with Free Home Sizers & Generous Resizing

If you want to buy the actual ring right away but just guess the size roughly, online-first and hybrid jewelers make the aftermath painless:

Free Sizing Kits: Many online brands will post you a complimentary plastic ring-measuring belt before you order, or automatically dispatch one with your order confirmation so you can double-check before they set the stone.

Free Resizing Windows: Most reputable UK independent and online jewelers offer a complimentary ring resizing window (usually between 30 to 60 days) if you get the initial guess wrong. (Just check whether they charge for complex styles like full-eternity bands, which can't always be easily resized).

3. The "Borrow a Ring" Sneaky Trick

If you want to present the real ring on day one without spoiling the surprise, you don’t necessarily need her exact size upfront:

Take one of her existing rings (ideally one she wears on her ring finger, or middle finger if you can adjust slightly) and press it firmly into a bar of soap or a piece of blu-tack to get an imprint, or slide it onto your own finger and mark how far down it goes.

Take that ring or measurement to a local independent jeweler or high-street store (like H.Samuel, Ernest Jones, or a local Hatton Garden jeweller), and they can usually size it by eye or using a mandrel.

Recommendation: If you're stressed about getting it completely wrong, go with a high street jeweler offering a proposal/placeholder ring policy or choose an online retailer with a free 30-day resizing guarantee.
