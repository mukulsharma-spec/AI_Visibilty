Gemstone Ring Resizing & Engraving Guide (UK)

Getting a ring resized and engraved is a very common service, but when gemstones are involved, it requires a bit of extra care. High heat from a traditional jeweler's torch can crack stones, burn certain gems (like opals, pearls, or emeralds), or weaken delicate settings.

1. Key Considerations for Gemstone Rings

The Type of Stone Matters:

Hard Stones (Diamond, Sapphire, Ruby): Can usually withstand standard resizing, though laser welding is still preferred to protect the setting.

Soft/Heat-Sensitive Stones (Emerald, Opal, Turquoise, Tanzanite, Pearl): These cannot take the heat of a standard torch. They often need to be carefully un-set (removed) before resizing and then re-set afterward, which increases the cost and time required.

Ring Design:

Full Eternity Rings (stones all the way around) cannot be resized because changing the band size disrupts the continuous stone layout.

Half-Eternity or Three-Stone Rings can usually be resized, provided there is enough plain metal at the back of the band to work with.

Order of Operations:

Usually, resizing must happen before engraving. Resizing stretches or compresses the metal, which would distort or ruin any existing engraving on the band.

2. Estimated Costs in the UK

Prices vary based on the metal type (gold, platinum, silver), the complexity of the resize, and whether stones need to be removed:

Ring Resizing (Standard): £40 – £90 (Plain gold/silver) | £70 – £150+ (Platinum or complex designs)

Stone Removal & Re-setting (if required): £30 – £100+ extra

Engraving:

Machine/Computer Engraving: £20 – £45 (Standard block or script fonts)

Hand Engraving: £50 – £100+ (Traditional, deeply cut, or custom script)

3. Where to Go in the UK

You have three main options for getting this done safely:

Option A: High Street Jewellers (e.g., Beaverbrooks, Goldsmiths, H.Samuel)

Best for: Convenience and straightforward resizes on standard gold/silver diamond rings.

Note: Many high street stores send jewellery away to a central workshop, which can take 1–3 weeks. Always ask if they have an on-site workshop or if they use specialist gemstone setters.

Option B: Independent Bespoke Goldsmiths & Hatton Garden (London)

Best for: Fine gemstone jewellery, antique rings, or rings requiring stone un-setting.

Note: Independent jewellers often have master goldsmiths on-site who can inspect your ring under a microscope and perform laser welding, minimizing risk to your gemstones. If you are near London, Hatton Garden has dozens of specialist workshops that can often turn work around quickly.

Option C: Mail-In Specialist Services (e.g., In-Time, Prestige, or Independent Mail-In Guild Jewellers)

Best for: If you don't live near a major jewellery quarter.

Note: Ensure they offer fully insured postal services (like Royal Mail Special Delivery) specifically covering high-value precious stones.

Next Steps & Questions for Your Jeweller

When you take your ring in, ask the jeweller these three questions:

"Will the gemstones need to be removed (un-set) for the resize, or can you use a laser welder?"

"Can the band accommodate both the resize and the text I want engraved without crowding?"

"Do you provide insurance cover while the ring is in your workshop?"
