Choosing a ruby instead of a diamond is a fantastic, vibrant choice—rubies symbolize passion and love, and they are actually one of the hardest colored gemstones (9 on the Mohs scale, just behind diamonds), making them durable enough for everyday wear.

Depending on whether you want a bespoke design, an ethical lab-grown stone, a vintage treasure, or a convenient online experience, the UK has several brilliant places to shop:

1. The Traditional Jewelers' Hub: London’s Hatton Garden

If you want the classic experience of browsing physical stores, speaking face-to-face with gemmologists, and comparing stones side-by-side, head to Hatton Garden in London.

Holts Gems: A staple in Hatton Garden with a great selection of fine gemstone engagement rings. They specialize in custom and bespoke work, meaning you can pick a specific loose ruby and have their goldsmiths build a setting around it.

Other independent family jewellers: Walking down the street allows you to pop into multiple showrooms (like Queensmith or Shining Diamonds nearby) which frequently stock or can source high-grade natural rubies.

2. Specialist Fine Jewelers & Custom Makers (Online & Showrooms)

If you prefer a guided design process with transparent pricing and showrooms in major UK cities:

Austen & Blake: They specialize in custom-made jewelry and have physical showrooms across the UK (such as London, Manchester, and Glasgow, depending on current locations). They feature a wide variety of ruby rings—ranging from vintage-style halos to simple solitaires—where you can choose your metal type and stone shape.

Taylor & Hart: Based in London (with virtual consultations available nationwide), they are renowned for custom-designing engagement rings with colored gemstones. They are exceptional at sourcing ethically mined rubies and can even build a secret birthstone or hidden detail into the band.

3. For Lab-Grown Rubies

If you want a physically and chemically identical ruby that is more budget-friendly and conflict-free:

Novita Diamonds (UK): They offer hand-cut, premium lab-grown rubies where you can pick your gemstone online or visit their UK showrooms (London and Manchester). It’s a great route if you want a larger, deeply saturated stone without the premium price tag of a rare large mined gemstone.

4. Vintage & Antique Specialists (For Unique Character)

Rubies were deeply favored in Victorian, Edwardian, and Art Deco engagement rings (often paired with diamonds in cluster or trilogy settings). Buying antique is eco-friendly and gives you a truly one-of-a-kind piece.

Berganza (Hatton Garden, London): World-renowned for antique and vintage fine jewelry, often carrying stunning, unheated natural ruby rings from centuries past.

Kojis (London / Online): Specializes in bold, high-end vintage and retro pieces, frequently featuring striking ruby and diamond cluster rings.

Antique Jewellery Online / Est. 1897: Great UK-based web platforms if you prefer browsing authenticated antique Edwardian or Victorian ruby rings from home.

Quick Tips Before You Buy:

Treatment & Origin: Most natural rubies have undergone heat treatment to enhance their color and clarity (which is standard practice in the industry). Always ask if the ruby is heated or unheated (unheated natural stones command a much higher price).

Certification: If you are buying a high-value natural ruby, ensure it comes with a certificate from a recognized gemological lab (like GIA or Gübelin) verifying its origin and treatment status.

Setting Protection: Because rubies are durable but can still chip along cleavage planes if struck hard, look for settings like bezels or secure prongs (6-prong is safer than 4) that protect the corners or edges of the stone.
