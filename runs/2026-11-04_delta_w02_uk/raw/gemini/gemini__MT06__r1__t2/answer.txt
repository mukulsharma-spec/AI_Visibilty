At the £1,500 and under price point, major high street jewellers (F. Hinds, Ernest Jones) and standard online retailers generally do not include an independent, third-party laboratory certificate (like GIA or IGI) for coloured gemstone pieces.

Instead, how certification and authentication work across these options depends on the source:

1. High Street & Online Retailers (F. Hinds, Ernest Jones, The Diamond Store)

What you get: They provide an in-house certificate of authenticity or a receipt valuation detailing the metal purity (hallmarked in the UK) and estimated carat weights/grades of the stones.

Why: Independent gemological lab reports (which can cost £100–£200+ on their own) are usually reserved for high-value center stones—typically rings starting north of £2,000 to £3,000.

The exception: If you buy a higher-end piece or customize a ring online at The Diamond Store, they sometimes offer specific grading options or certificates for larger central diamonds, but smaller accent rubies are company-verified rather than lab-certified.

2. What is officially certified on all of them?

Even without a separate gemstone certificate, the precious metal is legally certified. By UK law, any gold or platinum ring sold over a certain weight in the UK must carry a UK Hallmark stamped by an official Assay Office (such as London, Birmingham, or Sheffield). This guarantees the purity of the gold (e.g., 9ct or 18ct).

3. What about Vintage and Pre-Owned (Ramsdens, Antique Shops)?

Most vintage and antique ruby rings rarely come with original lab certificates unless they are high-end estate pieces.

Pre-owned jewellers like Ramsdens will guarantee that the gemstone is a natural ruby and the metal is genuine, backed by their own retail appraisal, but you would need to pay an independent lab (like the AnchorCert gem lab in Birmingham) if you wanted an official certificate after purchase.

Key Takeaway

If having an official, independent lab report for the ruby is a dealbreaker for you, you will either need to:

Contact an online retailer to see if they can add a lab certification for an extra fee.

Look for independent boutique jewellers who specialize in ethical/certified colored gemstones, though staying under £1,500 with a full lab report can be quite tight.
