Loose stone plus custom setting
If you want more control over the stone and the budget, buy a loose sapphire and have a jeweler set it. GemSelect sells loose sapphires online and has strong customer reviews. For a custom build, a local independent jeweler works well. For example, Laura Preshong in Boston offers custom design, including for out-of-state clients. For antique or vintage pieces, Fred Leighton is a high-end option.

What to check before buying

Natural vs. lab-grown. Both are sold, and lab-grown costs far less. Make sure the listing says which one it is.
Certification. For anything over a few hundred dollars, ask for a lab report from a gemological lab such as GIA, AGL or GRS. One shopping guide flagged a ring that came with no third-party gemological certificate.
Treatment disclosure. Most sapphires are heat-treated, which is normal. Untreated stones cost much more, so the seller should say which you’re getting.
Return policy and warranty. Look for a reasonable return period and free returns. A lifetime warranty on the setting is also worth having.
Durability. Sapphire is 9 on the Mohs scale, which makes it very good for daily wear.
Color. Color varies a lot from stone to stone, so compare several if you can.
