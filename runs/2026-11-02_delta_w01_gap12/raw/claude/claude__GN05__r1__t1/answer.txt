Listing recommended jewelers and noting ownership changes.

There isn’t one best place, and most “best of” lists online are written by retailers or affiliate sites, so I’d treat them with some skepticism. The right choice depends on your budget and what you’re buying.

For the widest selection of colored gemstone jewelry

Angara specializes in colored stones and has a huge range of sapphires, rubies, emeralds, tanzanite, morganite and more, in settings you can customize. It’s a good middle-of-the-road choice for value.
Ross-Simons is good for affordable, ready-made gemstone pieces, and it runs frequent sales.
Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth are reliable and have good return policies. However, colored gemstones are a secondary focus for Blue Nile next to diamonds, and Brilliant Earth’s selection is concentrated in sapphires, rubies and emeralds.

For unique or handmade pieces

Etsy has the most variety and many independent designers, but quality varies widely. Stick to sellers with lots of reviews and clear stone details.

What matters more than which site you pick

Treatment disclosure. Most sapphires, rubies and emeralds are heated or oil-treated. That’s normal, but it should be stated, and it affects price.
Natural vs. lab-created. Both are legitimate, but the retailer should say which one it is, and lab-created stones should cost much less.
Certification for expensive stones. For a stone costing a few thousand dollars or more, ask for a GIA or AGL report.
Return policy. Color looks different in person than on screen, so look for at least 30 days to return.
Real photos or video. Avoid listings that show only stock images, especially on marketplaces.
