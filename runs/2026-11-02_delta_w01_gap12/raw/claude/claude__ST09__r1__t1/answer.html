Several reputable US-based online retailers carry yellow sapphire rings, depending on what you're looking for.

Colored-gemstone specialists

Angara focuses on colored gemstones and has a large selection of yellow sapphire rings in different settings, metals, and quality grades.

The Natural Sapphire Company has one of the largest inventories of loose sapphires, including many yellow ones. You can pick a stone and have it set, which is good if you want control over the exact gem or an unheated stone.

Major fine-jewelry retailers

Brilliant Earth offers yellow sapphire engagement and fashion rings and emphasizes responsible sourcing.

Blue Nile and James Allen carry sapphire rings and let you customize settings, though their yellow selection is often smaller than their blue.

Mall and department-store brands

Kay, Zales, Helzberg, and Macy's sell more affordable options online, often in pre-set designs.

Independent makers

Etsy has many small jewelers who do custom yellow sapphire work. Quality varies, so check reviews, return policies, and whether the stone comes with certification.

What to check before you buy

Natural or lab-created: Lab-grown sapphires are real sapphires and cost much less, but they're worth less if you care about rarity or resale.

Heat treatment: Most yellow sapphires are heated to improve color, and this is standard. Unheated stones cost significantly more. Sellers should disclose this. Unheated stones matter most if you're buying for astrological reasons.

Certification: For higher-value stones, look for a report from GIA, AGL, or GRS confirming the stone's origin and treatment.

Return policy: Color can look different on screen, so a 30-day return window is a big plus.
