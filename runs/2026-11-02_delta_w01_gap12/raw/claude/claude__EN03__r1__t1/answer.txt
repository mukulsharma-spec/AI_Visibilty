Sapphire is a great choice. At 9 on the Mohs scale it’s second only to diamond in hardness, so it handles daily wear well. Here’s where to look in the US.

Large online retailers (easiest starting point)

Brilliant Earth has a big sapphire selection, including ethically sourced stones (Montana, Sri Lanka) and lab-created options. They also offer free resizing and good customer service.
Blue Nile and James Allen let you pick a loose sapphire and set it in a ring. Their sapphire inventory is smaller than their diamond inventory, but the build-your-own tools are easy to use.
Ritani has finished sapphire engagement rings and good customization options.

Independent and design-focused jewelers

Catbird (Brooklyn) is known for delicate, modern sapphire rings.
Etsy has independent jewelers who make custom sapphire rings, often at lower prices than big retailers. Check reviews and ask about certificates before buying.
Local independent jewelers can source stones and make custom rings, and you can see the stone in person. This is worth doing even if you buy elsewhere, just to learn what good color looks like.

Loose stones (if you want a custom setting)

Pala International (California) is a respected gem dealer.
Gem auction sites can offer good value, but only buy stones with lab reports.

What to know before buying

Color matters most. Look for a vivid, medium-to-medium-dark blue. Cornflower and royal blue are the classics. Stones that are too dark look inky, and too light look washed out.
Treatment and origin: Most sapphires are heat-treated, which is normal and accepted. Unheated stones cost significantly more. Sri Lankan (Ceylon), Burmese, Kashmir, and Montana origins carry premiums, so a lab report from GIA, AGL, or SSEF is worth it on pricier stones.
Cut: A well-cut stone looks bright and evenly colored. Avoid ones with a “window” (a see-through center) or dark, dead zones.
Lab-grown sapphires are chemically identical to natural ones and much cheaper, if budget matters more than natural origin.
Budget: Quality natural sapphires usually run about $500 to $3,000 per carat for good commercial quality, and far more for top-tier or unheated stones.
