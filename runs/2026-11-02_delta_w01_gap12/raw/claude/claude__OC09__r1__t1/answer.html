Emerald is the traditional gem for the 55th anniversary, so you have plenty of good options. Where to buy depends on your budget and how much you want to customize.

Major jewelers (reliable quality, easy returns)

Blue Nile and James Allen have large online selections of emerald rings, pendants, and earrings, with certification details listed. Brilliant Earth offers emerald pieces in classic and modern settings, with a focus on ethically sourced stones. Tiffany & Co., Cartier, and Mikimoto are the high-end choices if the budget is generous. Kay, Zales, and Jared are accessible mid-range options with in-store help.

Mid-range online and specialty

Ross-Simons and Angara often have good prices on emerald jewelry, including lab-created options. Etsy is good for personalized or vintage-style pieces. Check seller reviews and ask about stone origin and treatment.

Vintage and estate

1stDibs and local estate jewelers are good for a gift with history, especially fitting after 55 years together. Doyle, Bonhams, and Christie’s are options if you want to look at auction pieces.

What to look for when buying

Treatment disclosure. Most natural emeralds are oiled or filled to improve clarity. This is normal, but the seller should disclose it. Color over clarity. A vivid, rich green matters more than a flawless stone, since emeralds almost always have visible inclusions. Durability. Emeralds are more brittle than diamonds or sapphires, so a protective setting like a bezel or halo works well for a ring worn daily. Pendants and earrings are lower-risk. Certification. For pricier stones, ask for a report from GIA, AGL, or a similar lab. Lab-created emeralds have the same chemical makeup and look, at a fraction of the cost, if budget is a concern.
