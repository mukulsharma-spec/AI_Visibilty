At $500 you can get a genuine ruby necklace, but it will be small stones or a lower-grade color. Here is where I’d look.

Reliable online jewelers

Angara: specializes in colored gemstones, with many ruby pendants in the $200-$500 range. Their quality grading is clear and they offer returns. Ross-Simons: frequent sales, and ruby pendants in sterling silver or 14k gold often fall under $500. Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth: better selection and transparency, but the ruby options at this price are fewer and smaller. Jewelry Television (JTV) and Gemporia: lots of affordable natural rubies. Check the treatment disclosures carefully. Etsy: good for independent designers, but vet the seller (reviews, return policy, whether they provide a gem report).

Local options: Costco and independent jewelers sometimes have good deals, and a local jeweler lets you see the stone’s color in person.

What to check before buying

Treatment disclosure: Nearly all natural rubies are heat-treated, which is normal and acceptable. Avoid “glass-filled” or “lead-glass-filled” rubies. They are heavily treated, fragile, and worth very little, and some sellers don’t make this clear. Natural vs. lab-created: Lab-created rubies are chemically real rubies, often look cleaner, and cost far less. If you want a bigger, brighter stone for $500, this is the best value. If you want a natural stone, expect something smaller. Metal: Sterling silver or 10k/14k gold keeps the price in range. Return policy: Look for at least 30 days, so you can have it appraised independently. Be wary of Amazon and eBay: Fake or glass-filled stones are common, so only buy from sellers with a clear gem description and a solid return policy.

Prices and sales change often, so compare current listings before you buy. If you tell me whether you prefer natural or lab-created and what style you like (solitaire pendant, halo, tennis-style), I can narrow things down.
