Aquamarine is a lovely choice. It’s durable enough for daily wear (7.5-8 on the Mohs scale) and a good alternative to a diamond. Here are some solid places to look:

Online retailers with good selection

Brilliant Earth has a large aquamarine range, including settings with diamond accents, and its sourcing is transparent.
Blue Nile and James Allen let you pick a loose gemstone and a setting separately, so you can customize.
Gemvara is built around custom gemstone rings, so you choose the stone, metal, and style.
Catbird and Mejuri have a more delicate, modern look, though their engagement-style aquamarine options are more limited.

Independent and custom jewelers

Etsy has many independent jewelers who make aquamarine engagement rings, often at lower prices than big retailers. Check reviews carefully, and ask for a gem certificate or details about treatment and origin.
A local independent jeweler can source or design a custom ring, and you can see the stone’s color in person. This is often the best route if you want something unique or a specific shade.

What to look for

Color: The deeper, more saturated blues cost more, and pale stones can look washed out in some lighting. Look at it in person or ask for videos in natural light.
Clarity: Aquamarine is usually very clean, so choose one that looks eye-clean with no visible inclusions.
Treatment: Most aquamarine is heat-treated to improve color. That’s standard and stable, but the seller should disclose it.
Setting: It’s hard enough for everyday wear, but a bezel or halo setting protects the edges better than a high, exposed prong setting.
Certification: For a significant stone, ask about a report from a lab like GIA or AGL.
