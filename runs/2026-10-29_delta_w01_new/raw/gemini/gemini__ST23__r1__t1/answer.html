Shopping for an aquamarine engagement ring in the USA gives you fantastic options, ranging from large online custom jewelers to unique independent designers. Because aquamarine is a softer gemstone than a diamond (ranking around 7.5 to 8 on the Mohs scale), you'll want to buy from a reputable jeweler who uses secure settings (like bezels or protective prongs) and offers quality stone grading.

The best places to look depend on the style you want:

1. Best for Customization & Vast Selection (Online & Showrooms)

  • Angara: One of the best online retailers for colored gemstone engagement rings. They let you pick your exact aquamarine quality grade (from Heirloom to Better), choose your preferred carat size, and select the metal (platinum, white, yellow, or rose gold). They also offer a wide variety of ring styles, from vintage-inspired halo settings to modern solitaires.

  • Brilliant Earth: Known primarily for ethical sourcing, they have a dedicated "Colored Gemstone" section where you can choose a natural aquamarine and pair it with a recycled precious metal setting. Their user-friendly online builder allows you to preview the ring design step-by-step.

2. Best for Vintage, Unique, and Indie Designs

  • Catbird (New York-based, ships nationwide): If you prefer something delicate, dainty, or delightfully unconventional, Catbird features fine jewelry and engagement rings with unique antique or minimalist aesthetics.

  • Etsy (Independent USA-based Makers): If you search for "USA-based shops" on Etsy, you can find incredible independent lapidaries and bench jewelers (such as MagiJewelry, Oore Jewelry, or various boutique studios) who specialize in nature-inspired, whimsical, or custom gemstone rings. Tip: Just verify that the seller is genuinely based in the US and uses natural or responsibly sourced stones.

3. Best for Local Custom Craftsmanship

  • Local Independent Jewelers: Visiting a local custom jeweler in your city is often one of the smartest routes for an aquamarine ring. They can source loose aquamarines for you to inspect in person (paying close attention to the depth of the blue color and clarity), and custom-design a protective setting tailored specifically to your hand.

Quick Tips for Buying an Aquamarine:

  • Color & Clarity: Look for a stone with good transparency. While icy, pale blue is classic, many people prefer stones with a slightly deeper saturation (often called "Santa Maria" aquamarine), which command a higher price. Because aquamarines have great clarity naturally, inclusions are easily spotted—so look for eye-clean stones.

  • The Setting Matters: Since aquamarine can scratch or chip more easily than a diamond over years of heavy daily wear, ask your jeweler about bezel settings or protective prongs to keep the corners safe, especially if you choose an emerald cut or pear shape.
