Choosing a ruby instead of a diamond is a stunning and unique choice! Rubies symbolize passion and vitality, and they have a rich history of being used in royal engagement rings (most famously, Princess Diana and Kate Middleton's sapphire ring actually sparked a huge trend for colored gemstone engagement rings, and ruby has always been a top contender).

Depending on whether you prefer shopping online with customization options, visiting specialized gemstone specialists, or looking at local high-street jewelers, several great avenues exist for finding a ruby engagement ring:

1. Specialized Gemstone & Custom Jewelers

If you want a vast selection of loose rubies so you can hand-select the exact shade (from deep "pigeon's blood" red to softer raspberry tones) and pair it with a custom setting, these specialists are ideal:

The Natural Ruby Company: A long-standing specialist dedicated entirely to natural rubies with a massive online inventory of loose stones and custom settings.

Frank Darling: Known for incredible design variety and a custom approach, they allow you to design bespoke rings and often feature alternative gemstones alongside their diamond selections.

Austen & Blake: Great for bespoke, made-to-order fine jewelry where you can specify a ruby center stone in various gold or platinum settings.

2. Modern Fine Jewelry & Artisan Brands

If you want something with a contemporary, vintage-inspired, or artistic aesthetic:

Kinn Studio: Specializes in modern-vintage and heirloom-inspired styles that feel timeless and unique.

Roen: Excellent for design-forward, contemporary settings and bespoke ruby or colored gemstone options.

3. High Street & Traditional Retailers

If you prefer to see the ring in person, try it on, and talk face-to-face with an expert:

Local Independent Jewelers: Often your best resource for colored gemstones. They can source certified rubies through their trade networks and design a completely custom setting for you.

Major High-Street Jewelers (such as Goldsmiths, H.Samuel, or regional equivalents): Many carry alternative gemstone collections or can order custom pieces featuring rubies through their design services.

Quick Tips for Buying a Ruby:

Durability: Rubies score a 9 on the Mohs scale of hardness (diamonds are a 10), making them one of the few colored stones durable enough for everyday wear on an engagement ring.

Look for Certificates: Ensure the ruby comes with a certificate from a recognized gemological lab (like the GIA or GRS) verifying whether it is natural and whether it has undergone any treatments (like heat treatment, which is standard and stable for rubies, versus fracture-filling, which requires more care).
