There are plenty of oval sapphire and diamond halo pendants around. Most of what my search turned up are US retailers, so here is how the options break down by budget, plus some London-specific ideas.

Lab-created, lower budget (roughly $360-$965)

Rosec Jewels has an oval-cut created blue sapphire halo pendant in sterling silver, at $449 (about 1.55 ct). [rosecjewels]

Allurez has an oval lab-grown blue sapphire and diamond halo pendant in 14K white gold, at $965. [allurez]

Natural sapphire, mid-range (roughly $1,400-$3,500)

Reeds Jewelers has a petite 14K white gold oval sapphire and diamond halo pendant at $1,395. [reedsjewelers]

Nazar’s & Co. has an 18K white gold oval blue sapphire pendant with a 16-diamond pavé halo, at $1,610. [nazarsandco]

Washington Diamond has a 14K white gold pendant with a 1.00 ct oval blue sapphire and about 0.32 ct of diamonds, at $3,459. [washingtondiamond]

King Jewelers has a Roman and Jules 14K white gold pendant with a 0.50 ct oval sapphire, at $2,375. [kingjewelers]

Higher end

Robinson’s Jewelers carries designer pieces, such as a Fana oval sapphire and diamond halo pendant, at around $3,000-$3,650. [robinsonsjewelers]

Some listings show as sold out, and shipping and import duties to the UK will add to these prices.

In the UK, I’d start with these, though I haven’t checked their current stock:

Hatton Garden in London has many independent jewellers who can sell you a ready-made piece or make one to order. This is often the best value for natural sapphires.
Berry’s Jewellers, Goldsmiths and Mappin & Webb are established UK retailers that usually stock sapphire halo pendants.
Blue Nile and Angara both ship to the UK and let you customise the stone, metal and size.

Check whether the sapphire is natural, lab-created or heat-treated. A lab-created sapphire looks the same but costs much less, and a listing should state clearly which it is. Also check whether the diamonds are natural or lab-grown, and ask for a certificate or hallmark for any gold piece.
