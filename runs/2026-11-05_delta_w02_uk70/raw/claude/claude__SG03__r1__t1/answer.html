Congratulations on the thoughtful gesture! Where you buy depends on budget and how personal you want it to feel.

Independent jewellers are a great option if you want guidance and something with character. Since you’re in London, Hatton Garden is the classic spot, with dozens of independent jewellers where you can compare stones in person, ask questions, and often negotiate or commission a custom piece.

Online jewellers usually give you a wider selection and more transparent pricing. Blue Nile, Angara, and Brilliant Earth are well-known names with detailed stone information, and many have good return policies, which matters if you’re unsure of her ring size. Etsy is worth a look for handmade or vintage pieces, but check seller reviews carefully.

High street and department store jewellers (Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, H. Samuel) are convenient and let you see pieces in person, though you’ll generally pay more for the same quality.

Whichever route you take:

Check what “real” means to you. Natural gemstones are mined, while lab-created ones are chemically identical and usually much cheaper. Both are real gemstones, but a good seller will label which is which.
Look for a certificate or clear stone details (type, size, treatments), especially for pricier stones.
Look for a hallmark on gold, silver, or platinum, which is legally required in the UK above certain weights.
Check the return and resizing policy, since guessing ring size is the usual hiccup.
