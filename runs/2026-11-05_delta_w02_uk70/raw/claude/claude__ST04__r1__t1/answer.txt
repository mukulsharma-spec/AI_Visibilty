Since you're in London, you have good options both online and in person.

In person
- Hatton Garden: London's jewellery district has dozens of independent jewellers. You can see stones in person, compare shades, and often get a custom ring made or resized.
- High-street chains: Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, Beaverbrooks, and similar stores usually stock blue topaz, though London blue specifically varies by season and branch. Call ahead.

Online
- Angara: a specialist in coloured gemstones with a wide range of London blue topaz rings in different settings and metals.
- Astley Clarke and Monica Vinader: UK brands with more contemporary, everyday-style blue topaz designs.
- Etsy: good for independent makers and custom or vintage-style pieces, though quality varies, so check seller reviews.
- Amazon and general marketplaces: cheaper, but check the metal, stone details, and return policy carefully.

Things to check before buying
- Treatment: virtually all London blue topaz is treated (irradiated and heated) to get its deep colour. That's standard and stable, but a good seller will say so.
- Shade: "London blue" is the deepest of the blue topaz shades (lighter ones are Sky and Swiss blue). Some listings label lighter stones as London blue, so compare photos in daylight.
- Metal: sterling silver is affordable, while 9ct or 18ct gold costs more but lasts longer.
- Returns: especially for online purchases, make sure you can return it if the colour isn't what you expected.
