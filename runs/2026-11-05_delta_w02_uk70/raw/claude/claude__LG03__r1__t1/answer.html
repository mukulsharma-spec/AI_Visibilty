There isn’t one “best” retailer, since it depends on your budget and whether you want a plain stone or a statement piece. These are the UK options that came up, roughly by price tier:

  • Juvetti (London) is the budget-friendly end. Their Margo ruby channel band starts from £250, and they sell stacking bands and ruby-and-diamond designs in gold. juvetti
  • Angara UK is mid-range with certified stones. Their vintage-style bezel-set oval ruby ring in white gold is £1,709 including VAT. A lab-grown ruby halo engagement ring in 14ct yellow gold listing shows a GIA certificate and 0% instalment plans. angara angara
  • Lily Arkwright is the premium end, with platinum and 18k settings. Examples are a lab-grown ruby solitaire in a platinum 950 mount and ruby halo rings set with diamonds. lilyarkwright

Some points apply wherever you buy:

  • Ask for a grading report. A GIA or similar certificate on the ruby is a good sign, and Angara’s listing shows one.
  • Check the setting. Ruby is hard (Mohs 9), but a secure prong or bezel setting matters for everyday wear. rosecjewels
  • Compare the metal and the side stones. Much of the price gap between rings comes from platinum versus gold and from diamond accents, not the ruby itself.
  • Check the return policy. Rubies vary in colour on screen, so a decent returns window is worth having.

These are the retailers’ own pages, so I can’t vouch for their quality or service. Before buying, check independent reviews such as Trustpilot or Google.
