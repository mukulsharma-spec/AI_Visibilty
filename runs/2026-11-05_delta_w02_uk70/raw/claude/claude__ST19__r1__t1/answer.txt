Here are the main places to look, depending on what you want.

Specialist gemstone jewelers

Angara focuses on colored gemstones and lets you customize the setting, metal, and stone quality.
Blue Nile, James Allen, and Brilliant Earth are better known for diamonds, but all carry ruby rings and offer detailed photos or video.
Ruby Lane and 1stDibs are good for vintage and antique rings.

Budget-friendly and independent options

Etsy has many independent jewelers and custom makers. Quality varies a lot, so check reviews and seller history.
Amazon and Mejuri have lower-priced and lab-created options.

Luxury

Cartier, Tiffany, Graff, and Bulgari are the high-end route, with matching prices.

What to check before buying

Natural vs. lab-created: Lab-created rubies are chemically identical but much cheaper. A good seller states which one you’re getting.
Treatment: Most natural rubies are heat-treated, which is normal. Be cautious with “glass-filled” stones, which are lower value and fragile.
Certification: For anything expensive, look for a report from GIA, AGL, or GRS.
Return policy: Look for at least 30 days, since ruby color can look different in person than in photos.
Color: A vivid, slightly purplish red is the most prized. Very dark or brownish stones are generally worth less.
