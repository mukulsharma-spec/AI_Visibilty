The 55th is the emerald anniversary, so you have plenty of options. Since you’re in London, here are some good places to look.

In London

Hatton Garden is the traditional jewellery district, with dozens of independent jewellers. You can often negotiate, ask for custom work, and see stones side by side. Its dealers and workshops are good for loose emeralds or a bespoke piece.
Bond Street and Mayfair have the high-end names, such as Graff, Boodles and Garrard, for a luxury piece.
Antique and vintage dealers at places like Burlington Arcade or Grays Antique Market are good for older emerald pieces, which suit a long marriage.

Online

Established retailers such as Berry’s Jewellers, Goldsmiths, Mappin & Webb or Ernest Jones tend to have emerald rings, pendants and earrings, with clear returns policies.
Specialist gemstone sellers offer more choice of stone quality.

Tips for buying

Emeralds almost always have natural inclusions, so don’t expect a flawless stone. Look for rich, even green colour, and ask whether the stone has been treated, since oil or resin filling is common.
For a stone of any real value, ask for a gemmological report from a lab like SSEF, Gübelin or GIA.
Emeralds are softer and more brittle than diamonds, so a protective setting such as a bezel or halo suits a ring worn daily. Pendants and earrings are lower-risk choices.
Check the seller is a member of the National Association of Jewellers or the Gem-A, and get a valuation certificate for insurance.
