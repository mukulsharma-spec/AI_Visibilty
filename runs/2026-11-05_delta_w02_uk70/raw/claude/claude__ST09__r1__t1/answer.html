Here are some good places to start, depending on what you want:

Specialist colored gemstone jewelers

  • Angara has a large selection of yellow sapphire rings in many settings, ships internationally, and lets you customize the metal and stone.
  • Brilliant Earth and Blue Nile both have UK sites. They carry fewer yellow sapphires, but their quality standards and return policies are solid.

UK options

  • Hatton Garden jewelers in London are good if you'd like to see stones in person or commission a custom piece. Many also sell online.
  • Gemporia is a UK gemstone retailer with a wide range of prices, though quality varies, so read the stone descriptions carefully.

Independent sellers

  • Etsy has many sellers offering one-of-a-kind and vintage-style rings, often at lower prices. Check seller reviews and ask for lab documentation before buying.

Before you buy

  • Treatment: Most yellow sapphires are heat-treated, which is standard and accepted, but the seller should say so. Unheated stones cost noticeably more.
  • Certification: For anything beyond a few hundred pounds, look for a report from a lab like GIA, SSEF, or GRS.
  • Color: Look for a vivid, evenly saturated yellow. Very pale or brownish stones are usually cheaper for a reason.
  • Natural vs. lab-grown: Lab-grown yellow sapphires are real sapphire (same chemistry), look the same, and cost far less. Just make sure the listing says which one you're getting.
  • Returns: Pick a seller with a clear return window and free resizing, since ring sizes are easy to get wrong online.
