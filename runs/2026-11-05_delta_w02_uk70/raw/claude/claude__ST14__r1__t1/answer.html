Emeralds are tricky to shop for because quality varies hugely and “cheap” often means heavily treated or poorly cut stones. Here are the places I’d look, roughly by how they fit different budgets:

Best value for natural emeralds

  • Angara: Good selection of natural emerald rings and pendants in sensible price brackets, with clear quality grades. Not the absolute cheapest, but you can see what you’re paying for.
  • Ross-Simons: Often has sales, and its emerald pieces tend to be priced well for the quality.
  • Gemporia (UK-based): Strong for gemstones in general, with lots of lower-cost natural emerald pieces, though quality varies, so read the treatment details.

For a nicer or more custom piece

  • Etsy (established sellers with lots of reviews): Often the best prices, since independent jewelers have lower overhead. Check that they offer a gem report and a return policy.
  • Brilliant Earth and James Allen: Better for engagement-style rings. They cost more, but the stones and settings are reliable.

Budget option

  • Lab-grown emeralds: Look for these if you want a rich green at a fraction of the price. They’re chemically identical to natural ones and often cleaner, but they won’t hold resale value.

Tips to avoid overpaying

  • Oil or resin filling is normal for emeralds. Moderate treatment is fine, but pass on anything described as heavy or significant.
  • For stones over about 1 carat, ask for a lab report (GIA, AGL, or Gübelin are the gold standard).
  • Color matters most. A vivid, slightly bluish green is worth paying for, while pale or very dark stones are cheaper for a reason.
  • Beware of “too good to be true” listings that don’t say whether the stone is natural, lab-grown, or a simulant.
