Garnet is easy to find in the UK, and the right place depends mainly on your budget.

Mid-range high street and online jewellers

Ramsdens has a large garnet range, mostly 9ct yellow gold solitaires, clusters, and signet rings. Prices on the listing run from about £129 to over £400, with free UK delivery and a 14-day return policy. [ramsdensjewellery]

F. Hinds carries garnet rings from around £60 for silver up to several hundred pounds for 9ct gold, including garnet-and-diamond birthstone styles. Collection in store is available. [fhinds]

Specialist gemstone sellers

C W Sellors and W Hamond are family-run, gemstone-focused jewellers. W Hamond describes itself as a British gemstone specialist dating back to 1860. They are a good choice if you want something more distinctive than a standard high street ring. [whamond]

High end

Pragnell sells rare garnet varieties such as rhodolite and demantoid. Its pieces are priced from several thousand pounds upward. This is a different tier from the options above. [pragnell]

Since you’re in London, Hatton Garden is also worth a visit. It has dozens of independent jewellers, and many will do custom work, so you can see stones in person and compare quality.

What to look for

Color: Deep, even red or wine tones are classic. Garnets also come in orange (spessartite), green (tsavorite, demantoid), and pink-purple (rhodolite), if she likes something less traditional.
Durability: Garnet is slightly softer than sapphire, so a protective setting such as a bezel or low-profile design is better for daily wear.
Her style: Check what metal she usually wears, yellow or white gold, so the ring matches the rest of her jewellery.
