Chatham is the best-known name for lab-grown sapphire, and several online jewelers sell its stones.

Retailers I found:

- Chatham sells its own lab-grown sapphire rings and fine jewelry, backed by a lifetime warranty and a company history going back to 1938. Its pieces are priced as fine jewelry, so expect high prices on the designer settings. [chatham]
- Lily Arkwright is a UK jeweler that describes itself as the UK's first official Chatham stockist, with Chatham authentication for its lab-grown sapphires. Its listings include a Chatham lab-grown pink sapphire solitaire in platinum at £829. It also carries blue, yellow, and champagne sapphires, mostly in engagement-ring styles. [lilyarkwright] [lilyarkwright]
- Angara has a dedicated lab-grown blue sapphire section, with round, oval, pear, and cushion shapes in rose or yellow gold, platinum, white gold, or silver. It also offers customization. [angara]
- Independent jewelers selling Chatham pieces, such as Leo Hamel and Avant Garde Jewelers in the US, list rings like a 14k white gold halo ring with a 2.85 carat lab-grown sapphire for $3,125. Check their shipping to the UK before ordering. [leohamel]

If you want a loose stone for a custom design: one buyer on a gem forum found a Chatham lab sapphire of 4.5 carats for $1,188. That post is old, so treat the price as a rough guide only. [pricescope]

Before you buy:

- Ask for a certificate of authenticity or lab report, and check the seller labels the stone as "lab-grown" or "lab-created."
- Compare the metal (sterling silver, 10k, 14k, 18k, or platinum) and any return policy.
- Prices range widely, from budget silver pendants to gold and platinum fine jewelry.

Most of what comes up online is for lab-grown diamonds, so sapphire-specific selection is narrower. If you tell me what you're after (ring, necklace, earrings), your budget, and your preferred color, I can narrow this down.
