What “actually real” means with sapphires
Natural vs lab-created: Lab-created sapphires are chemically real sapphire, but they’re worth far less. Look for the word “natural” on the listing, not just “sapphire”. Some sellers use the bare word for either.
Treatment: Most natural sapphires are heat-treated, which is normal and accepted. The seller should disclose it, and fully untreated stones cost much more. At this budget, heated natural stones are what you’ll realistically get.
Hallmark: UK gold over 1g must carry a hallmark (9ct is 375). It confirms the metal, not the stone, but it’s a good sign of a legitimate seller.
Sellers who say less: If a listing won’t say natural or created, ask them to confirm in writing. A reputable jeweller will answer straight away.
Price expectations: Under £1,000, you’ll mostly find small stones (about 3–5mm) in 9ct gold. Larger stones usually mean lab-created, heavily included, or very pale. If a pair of big, vivid blue sapphires costs a couple of hundred pounds, be sceptical.
