Generally, a specialist is the better bet for colored gemstones, though it depends on what you’re buying and how much you’re spending.

Why specialists tend to win
- Selection and sourcing. Specialists often buy directly from cutters or mines, so you can compare many stones of the same type and see real differences in color, clarity, and cut. Chains usually carry a narrow, pre-selected range.
- Expertise. Colored stones are far more variable than diamonds. Origin, treatments (heat, oil, diffusion, irradiation), and color saturation all affect value heavily, and a good specialist can explain these. Chain staff are often trained mainly on diamonds and sales.
- Disclosure. Reputable specialists are upfront about treatments and will often provide lab reports from labs like GIA, AGL, Gübelin, or SSEF for higher-value stones.
- Pricing. With fewer layers of overhead and brand markup, you often get better quality for the money, especially for stones in the mid-to-high range.

When a chain can make sense
- You want a lower-priced piece (say, a simple birthstone ring) and care more about convenience than stone quality.
- You value a standardized return policy, warranty, or financing.
- You want a finished piece quickly and don’t want to source a stone and setting separately.

What to check either way
- Ask whether the stone is natural or lab-grown, and what treatments it has had. Get this in writing on the receipt.
- For anything expensive, ask for an independent lab report.
- Confirm the return policy, ideally with a window long enough to get an independent appraisal.
- Compare similar stones across a few sellers. Wide price gaps usually mean a difference in quality or treatment.

Red flags at any seller
- Vague answers about treatment or origin
- Pressure to buy immediately
- “Retail value” claims or appraisals that are suspiciously high compared to the price
