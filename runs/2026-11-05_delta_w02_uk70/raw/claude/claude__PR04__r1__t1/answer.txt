Cheaper alternatives
Lab-created emeralds give you the colour for far less. For example, Created Brilliance sells a 9ct gold created emerald and lab-grown diamond cluster ring for £700. Just make sure the listing says “created” or “lab-grown”, so you know exactly what you’re buying. [very]
Pre-owned and antique pieces often give you more emerald for the money. Jewellery auction houses and specialist antique dealers are worth a look, though fine antique pieces can run well over budget (one early-20th-century emerald ring was estimated at £2,000–£3,000 and sold for £6,048). [woolleyandwallis]
eBay has many listings, but quality varies widely. Stick to sellers with strong reviews and use the Authenticity Guarantee where it applies.

Tips for getting the most for your budget
Emerald prices vary greatly with cut, clarity and colour. Colour matters most, so look for a rich, even green rather than a pale or yellowish one. [prudenandsmith]
Almost all natural emeralds have visible inclusions, and that’s normal. They’re usually treated with oil, so ask about it.
Ask for a gemmological report or certificate, particularly for anything with a larger stone.
Smaller emeralds, 9ct gold and diamond accents rather than a large centre diamond will all stretch your budget further.
Check the returns policy. Some sellers, such as Michael Jones, only offer 14-day returns on certain items. [michaeljonesjeweller]
