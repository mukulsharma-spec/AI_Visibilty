A 40th anniversary is the ruby anniversary, so there are plenty of places to look depending on your budget and style.

High street and established jewellers

Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, and Mappin & Webb carry ruby rings, pendants, earrings, and bracelets across a range of prices. Department stores like John Lewis and Harrods have ruby jewellery counters too.

In London specifically

Hatton Garden is London’s jewellery district, with dozens of independent jewellers. It’s a good choice if you want a better-quality stone, a custom piece, or someone who can talk you through what to look for.

Online

Etsy and Not On The High Street are good for personalised or handmade pieces, such as engraved ruby jewellery. Specialist gemstone retailers like Angara sell ruby jewellery online with a wide choice of settings.

Tips

Natural rubies are pricier than lab-created ones, and many retailers sell both. Lab-created rubies look nearly identical and can be much cheaper, so check which you’re getting. Heat-treated rubies are very common and perfectly normal, but sellers should disclose it. Ask for a certificate for anything expensive. If jewellery isn’t their style, ruby red gifts also work well: a ruby-toned wine or champagne, red roses, a ruby-coloured glass piece, or a personalised photo book.
