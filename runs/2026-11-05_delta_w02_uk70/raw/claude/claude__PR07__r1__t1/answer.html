For a purchase at this level, the seller and the paperwork matter as much as the stone. Here are the main routes, roughly in order of what I’d consider first.

Specialist and antique dealers. These are often the best value and expertise for coloured stones. In London, Hancocks (Burlington Arcade) and Bentley & Skinner (Piccadilly) are well-regarded for fine coloured gemstones and period pieces, and they’ll usually discuss a stone’s origin, treatment and history in detail. Antique and vintage rings also tend to cost less than equivalent new pieces from a luxury house.

Established jewellers with in-house design. Boodles, Garrard, Pragnell and Mappin & Webb sell quality coloured stones with strong aftercare. You pay more for the brand and setting, but you get service, resizing and insurance support.

Auction houses. Sotheby’s, Christie’s, Bonhams and Phillips regularly sell sapphires, rubies, emeralds and spinels, often with lab reports, and prices can be lower than retail. The catch is that sales are final, so you need to be comfortable assessing a stone or have an expert advise you. Buyer’s premiums (often 25% or more) also add to the hammer price.

Gem dealers and custom work. Hatton Garden has gem dealers who can source a loose stone, which a jeweller then sets to your design. This gives the most control over quality and price, but it takes research and trust, so check how long a dealer has been trading and whether they’re recognised by trade bodies such as the National Association of Jewellers or the Gem-A community.

Whatever you choose, check these:

An independent lab report. For a stone over £5,000, ask for a report from a respected lab such as SSEF, Gübelin, GIA or AGL. The report should state the species, colour, any treatments (heat treatment is common and accepted, but affects value) and, where possible, geographic origin. Origin like Burma/Myanmar, Kashmir or Colombia can raise prices dramatically.

Honest treatment disclosure. Unheated, untreated stones command large premiums, and you should only pay those if the report supports it.

Return and valuation policy. Reputable sellers allow an independent valuation within a set window.

Insurance. Get a replacement valuation for the ring and check that your policy covers it.
