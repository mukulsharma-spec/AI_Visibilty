Here are a few examples of ruby studs in yellow gold. A few things to know when comparing them:

Styles you’ll see

Solitaire rounds: a single ruby per ear in a classic 4-prong or bezel setting. This is the simplest look and the most common.

Ovals and other shapes: these look a little more distinctive and often appear in vintage-inspired designs.

Diamond halos: a ring of small diamonds around each ruby makes the stone look bigger and adds sparkle.

Gold purity

14k is harder and more durable, so it suits everyday wear. It also has a slightly paler yellow tone.

18k has a richer, warmer color and a higher gold content. It’s a bit softer, though that matters little for studs.

10k is the most affordable and the toughest, but its color is lighter.

Ruby quality

Look for a vivid red, ideally with a slight hint of blue (often called “pigeon’s blood” at the top end). Very dark or orangey stones are less valued.

Most rubies are heat-treated, which is standard and accepted. Untreated stones cost much more, and a lab report is worth asking for.

Lab-created rubies look the same as natural ones and cost far less, so they’re a good option if you want a larger or more flawless stone on a budget.

Practical tips

Screw-back or locking friction backs are safer for expensive stones.

Total carat weight (ctw) covers both earrings. For example, 0.30 ctw means about 0.15 ct per ear.
