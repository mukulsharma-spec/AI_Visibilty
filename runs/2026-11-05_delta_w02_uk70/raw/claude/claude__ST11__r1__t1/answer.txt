There are a few good routes, depending on your budget and whether you want natural or lab-grown stones.

Online specialists (wide selection, often good prices)

Angara and Blue Nile both have large ruby pendant ranges with detailed stone information. Check that they ship to the UK and what the import and return terms are.
Etsy is good for independent makers and custom or vintage pieces. Check seller reviews and ask for a gemstone certificate for any pricier stone.

UK jewellers

Goldsmiths, Mappin & Webb, Beaverbrooks, and Ernest Jones stock ruby pendants, mostly in the mid to high range.
Hatton Garden in London is the diamond and jewellery district, with dozens of independent jewellers where you can see stones in person, negotiate, and often commission a custom piece.
Monica Vinader, Astley Clarke, and similar brands offer more affordable, fashion-oriented gemstone pendants.

Things to check before buying

Natural vs. lab-created: Lab-grown rubies are chemically identical but much cheaper. Retailers should state which you're getting.
Treatment: Most natural rubies are heat-treated, which is normal but should be disclosed. Untreated stones cost significantly more.
Certification: For stones of any real value, look for a report from a lab like GIA, IGI, or GRS.
Metal and setting: Look for hallmarked gold or silver, and check how securely the stone is set.
Returns: For online purchases, make sure there's a decent return window.
