It depends on your budget and whether you want natural or lab-created stones. Here are the options I’d look at, from my general knowledge (I haven’t checked current stock or prices).

Specialist online jewellers

Angara is probably the closest match, since ruby-and-diamond halo studs are a core part of their range. You can choose the metal, ruby quality, and size, and they ship internationally. Prices are mid-range, and sales and promo codes are frequent.
Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth are good for quality and transparent grading, though their coloured-gemstone selection is smaller.

UK high-street retailers

Goldsmiths, Beaverbrooks, Mappin & Webb, and Berry’s let you see the earrings in person and have easy returns. Prices tend to be higher, and the rubies are often fairly small.

Custom or independent

Hatton Garden jewellers in London can make a pair to your spec, often at good value for the quality. This is also a good route for ethically sourced stones or a specific ruby colour.
Etsy has independent makers with lots of variety, but check reviews and hallmarking carefully.

What to check before buying

Ruby treatment: Most natural rubies are heat-treated, which is normal but should be disclosed. Untreated stones cost far more.
Lab-grown vs natural: Lab-grown rubies and diamonds look identical and cost much less, so they’re worth considering if budget matters.
Metal and backs: 9k or 14k gold is more durable for everyday wear, and screw-backs are more secure than push-backs.
Returns policy: Look for at least 30 days, since the stone colour can look different in person.
