It depends on your budget and whether you want natural or lab-created stones, but here are some reliable options.

Specialists in colored gemstones: Angara focuses on colored stones (sapphire, emerald, ruby, and so on), offers both natural and lab-created options, and lets you customize metal and stone. Brilliant Earth and Blue Nile also carry gemstone tennis bracelets, though they’re better known for diamonds.

High street and mid-range (UK): Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, Beaverbrooks, and Mappin & Webb have in-person service and easy returns, which helps when you want to see how a bracelet sits on the wrist.

Bespoke or better value: Hatton Garden in London has many independent jewelers who can make or customize a bracelet, often at better prices than big retailers. Reputable independent makers on Etsy are another option, but check reviews carefully.

Whichever you choose, a few things matter more than the brand:

Stone treatment and origin: Most sapphires and emeralds are heated or oiled, which is normal, but the seller should disclose it, and it affects price. Lab-created stones cost much less for similar appearance.
Setting and clasp: Look for secure prongs or bezels and a box clasp with a safety latch. Tennis bracelets are notorious for being lost because of weak clasps.
Consistent color: In a bracelet, matched stones matter more than one standout stone, so look at photos or videos in natural light.
Return policy and certification: For higher-value pieces, ask for a grading report or appraisal and a decent return window.
