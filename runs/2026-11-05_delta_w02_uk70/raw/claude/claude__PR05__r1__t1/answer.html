Your £1,500 will go a long way if you’re realistic about size and set it up smartly. For scale, a 9ct white gold pendant with a 0.58ct natural ruby was listed at £279, down from £429, while a 3ct+ natural ruby and diamond pendant was £12,000 from an antiques dealer and over £7,600 from another retailer. So at your budget, think small-to-medium natural stones, or a different style of necklace.

Where to look

Online UK jewellers. The Fine Jewellery Company lists natural ruby pendants in 9ct and 18ct gold, including an 18ct white gold ruby and diamond cluster. Gemondo sells pieces like a 9ct white gold natural ruby and diamond pendant with a certificate of authenticity. These are good for comparing prices quickly.

Antique and vintage dealers. Often the best value for your money. One London dealer listed a natural ruby bead necklace, about 140 carats total, for £980 with a valuation certificate. Try LoveAntiques, Jewellery Discovery, and Portobello or Camden Passage markets.

Hatton Garden. Independent jewellers there can source a loose ruby and set it for you, which is a good route if you want a specific colour or design. Ask to see several stones and get a written description of the stone.

What to check before buying

Natural or lab-grown? Both are genuine rubies chemically, but lab-grown ones are far cheaper. For example, Juvetti sells lab-grown ruby pendants from around £420. If “real” means natural, make sure the listing says natural.

Treatment. Most natural rubies are heated, which is normal and accepted, but it should be disclosed. Be wary of fracture-filled or glass-filled stones sold cheaply, which are fragile and worth much less.

Certification. For a single stone over about 1ct, ask for a lab report (GIA, IGI, or similar) stating natural origin and treatment. For tiny accent stones, a seller’s certificate is more typical.

Hallmark. UK gold should carry an assay hallmark.

Ways to stretch the budget

Choose 9ct gold rather than 18ct.

Pick a smaller central ruby with diamond accents instead of a large single stone.

Consider a bead or multi-stone necklace, which gives you more visible ruby for the money.

Buy antique or pre-owned.

What style are you after, a single pendant, a beaded strand, or something more statement-like? That would help narrow down where to focus.
