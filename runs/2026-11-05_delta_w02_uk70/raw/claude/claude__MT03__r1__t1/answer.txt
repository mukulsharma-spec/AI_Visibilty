The best place depends on budget and how much you want to hand-pick the stone, but a few options are reliable:

Reputable online specialists (Angara, Blue Nile, Brilliant Earth, James Allen) offer big selection, clear photos and videos, usually 30-day returns, and stones with documented treatment. This is the easiest route for most people.

Independent or high-street jewellers with gemmologists let you see the stone in person, which matters for ruby since colour varies a lot with lighting. Jewellery districts like Hatton Garden in London are good for this, since you can compare many dealers and negotiate.

Auction houses and specialist dealers (Christie's, Sotheby's, or dealers who show lab reports) are best for high-end or untreated stones, where provenance really matters.

Whichever you choose, check these things:

Treatment disclosure. Heat treatment is standard and fine. Avoid "glass-filled" or "lead-glass-filled" rubies, which are fragile and worth very little, however good they look.
A lab report for anything over roughly £1,000-£2,000. GIA, SSEF, Gübelin and AGL are the trusted labs.
Return policy, so you can see the ring in daylight and compare it to your expectations.
Colour over size. A vivid, slightly purplish red ("pigeon's blood" at the top end) is worth far more than a larger, paler or brownish stone.
Origin if it matters to you. Burmese stones carry a premium, while Mozambique and Thai rubies are excellent value.
