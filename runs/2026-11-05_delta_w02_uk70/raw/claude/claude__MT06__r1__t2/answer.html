None of those options certify the stone. They were about the occasion, style and metal. Certification of the stone itself comes from independent gemmological labs. For rubies, the ones to look for are:

GIA, the most widely recognised, though it's better known for diamonds. Gübelin, SSEF and AGL, the top-tier labs for coloured stones. They also report origin (Burma, Mozambique, Thailand and so on), which affects price. GRS, IGI and Gem-A (UK), reputable and more affordable, and common at lower price points.

A few things matter at your budget:

Treatment disclosure. Most rubies are heat treated, which is normal and accepted. The thing to avoid is lead-glass filled rubies sold as natural. A report should state "heated" or "no indication of heating" and mention any filling. Hallmarks don't cover the stone. The UK hallmark (from an Assay Office) only certifies the metal's purity, not the ruby. Reports are rare under about £3,000. Many reputable jewellers sell stones under 1 carat with their own written description instead of a lab report. A lab report is realistic for a larger or higher-quality stone, and it adds cost. You can also pay for one yourself on a ring you've bought.
