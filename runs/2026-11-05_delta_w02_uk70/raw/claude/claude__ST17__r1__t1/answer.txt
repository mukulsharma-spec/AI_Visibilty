Here are some reliable places to start, grouped by what you're after.

Specialist gemstone jewellers

Angara has a very wide range of emerald studs, drops, and halos in different qualities and metals, and many pieces can be customised.
Brilliant Earth and Blue Nile focus on fine jewellery with detailed stone information and good return policies.
Gemporia and Fraser Hamilton suit a lower budget and sell lots of gemstone earrings, though quality varies, so check the details.

High street and heritage jewellers

Goldsmiths, Ernest Jones, and Mappin & Webb offer mainstream and higher-end emerald earrings with in-store backup.
Monica Vinader, Astley Clarke, and Mejuri sell more delicate, everyday-style pieces, often with smaller emeralds at moderate prices.

Independent makers

Etsy has one-off and vintage-style designs. Stick to sellers with lots of reviews and clear stone descriptions.

What to check before buying

Natural vs. lab-grown. Lab-grown emeralds look very similar but cost much less. Reputable sellers state which one it is.
Treatment. Nearly all natural emeralds are oiled to hide inclusions. That’s normal, but it should be disclosed.
Certification. For anything pricey, look for a report from a lab such as GIA or SSEF, or at least a clear grading description.
Colour and clarity. A rich, slightly bluish green with visible inclusions is typical. A completely flawless emerald is unusual and should make you cautious.
Returns and metal. Look for a 14-30 day return window, and check the metal (solid gold or sterling silver versus plated).
