Congratulations on the treat! The best place depends on what you want, so here’s a rough map of the options (I’m assuming you’re in London, so I’ve leaned UK).

Independent and bespoke jewellers
Hatton Garden is the classic choice. It’s London’s historic jewellery quarter, with dozens of independent shops and workshops, and you can often negotiate, see stones in person, or commission something custom. Brands like Boodles and Fenwick-type high street names sit at the more traditional end, while smaller designers on Bond Street, Burlington Arcade and Marylebone offer more distinctive pieces.

Online specialists
Angara is a good fit if you want a particular gemstone (sapphire, emerald, ruby, morganite and so on) in a customisable setting, with a wide range of stones and metals. Other options include Blue Nile for stone-focused buying and Etsy for independent makers, though quality varies more there.

Designer and mid-range brands
Pandora and Swarovski are more affordable but lean towards lab-created or simulated stones. Mid-range brands like Monica Vinader or Astrid & Miyu give you a fashion-forward look at moderate prices.

Vintage and antique
If you like character, look at Portobello Road, Camden Passage in Islington, or specialist auction houses and antique jewellers. You often get far more quality per pound, and no two pieces are alike.

A few things worth checking wherever you buy:

Whether the stone is natural, lab-created or treated (heat treatment is common and fine, but it should be disclosed)
A certificate from a recognised lab, such as GIA or SSEF, for more expensive stones
The return policy, especially online
Hallmarking on the metal, which is a legal requirement in the UK for gold, silver and platinum above small weight thresholds

To point you somewhere more specific, it would help to know your rough budget and which gemstone you have in mind. Do you already have a stone or style in mind?
