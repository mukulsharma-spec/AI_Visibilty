Here are some good places to look, depending on what you want:

Fine jewellery retailers
- Angara sells natural opal pendants in gold and platinum, with plenty of settings and customisation.
- Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth have smaller opal selections but are reputable on quality and returns.
- UK high-street jewellers like Beaverbrooks, Ernest Jones, Goldsmiths and H.Samuel often stock opal pendants, and many ship online.

Opal specialists
- Australian dealers such as Opal Minded, Black Star Opal and Opals Down Under focus on natural opals, including boulder and black opals, and often describe the stone's colour play in detail.

Marketplaces for variety and handmade pieces
- Etsy has the widest range and price spread, including independent designers. Check seller reviews and whether the opal is natural or lab-created.
- Amazon is fine for budget options, but be careful with listings that don't say what kind of opal it is.

Things to check before buying
- Natural vs. lab-created vs. synthetic. All can look beautiful, but the price should reflect which one it is.
- Solid opal vs. doublet or triplet. Doublets and triplets are thin slices of opal glued to a backing. They cost less but are less durable and can be damaged by water.
- Metal and hallmarks. Look for sterling silver (925) or gold hallmarks.
- Return policy. Opals vary a lot in colour, so a good return window helps if the stone looks different in person.
