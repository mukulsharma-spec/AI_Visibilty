January’s birthstone is garnet, usually a deep red, though it also comes in orange, green, and other colors. You have a few good options for buying it.

Online jewelers specializing in gemstones


Angara has a large selection of garnet rings, pendants, earrings, and bracelets, with options to customize the metal and stone quality.

Blue Nile and James Allen carry garnet pieces, though they focus more on diamonds.

Brilliant Earth offers garnet in more modern and ethically sourced designs.


Big retailers and department stores


Amazon and Etsy have a huge range of prices. Etsy is especially good for handmade or custom garnet jewelry, but check seller reviews and whether the stone is natural or lab-created.

Macy’s, Kay, Zales, and Jared (US), or Ernest Jones, H. Samuel, and Goldsmiths (UK), usually stock garnet in their birthstone collections.


Budget-friendly


Pandora and Swarovski sell birthstone charms and pieces if you want something affordable and giftable, though the stones are often simulated, not natural garnet.


Tips for buying


Check whether the listing says “natural garnet.” Many cheaper pieces use glass or synthetic stones.

Garnet is fairly durable (about 6.5 to 7.5 on the Mohs scale), so it works well for everyday rings and pendants.

Look for rich, even color without visible cloudiness.

Sterling silver is the best value for a gift. Gold costs more but suits fine jewelry.
