Several well-known online jewelers carry both. Most of them focus on diamonds, and a few also have colored gemstones.

Diamonds, natural and lab-grown

- Blue Nile is one of the original online jewelers. It offers both mined and lab-grown diamonds. One guide says it carries 300,000+ GIA-certified natural and lab-grown diamonds, with a price-match guarantee and physical showroom access in 9 U.S. states. [brilliantearth] [learningjewelry]
- James Allen is owned by Signet. Its natural diamonds come with GIA certification, and its lab-grown stones are IGI certified. It is also known for its 360-degree stone viewing. [culturaldaily]
- Brilliant Earth focuses on ethical sourcing and sustainability, with both lab and natural options. [parse]
- Ritani describes itself as an online seller of natural diamonds, lab-grown diamonds, and fine jewelry. It has also added colored lab diamonds. [presshook]
- Diamonds-USA provides buyers with good prices for both lab-grown and loose natural diamonds. [nerdbot]

Colored gemstones, natural and lab-created
Brilliant Earth’s own buying guide says top retailers offer a mix of natural and lab-created stones in colored gemstones. Brilliant Earth itself carries both, though that guide is the company’s own marketing, so it is worth checking the product pages yourself. Rosec Jewels also claims both natural and lab-grown diamonds together with an extensive selection of colored gemstones, but that claim comes from its own blog. [brilliantearth] [rosecjewels]

Outside the US

- Diamonds Factory is UK-based and offers both lab-grown and natural diamond jewelry, with international delivery. [culturaldaily]
- Antonelli Diamond is a Hong Kong specialist that offers both loose natural diamonds and lab-grown diamonds, as well as finished jewellery. [nny360]

Worth knowing

- Some retailers sell only lab-grown stones, such as Clean Origin, so they won’t help if you want both.
- Many “best of” lists are written by retailers or affiliates, so treat the rankings with some caution.
- Inventory changes often. Before buying, check that the specific stone type you want is available, and look for independent certification (GIA or IGI).
