What to look for when buying a ruby

This is particularly important for an engagement ring because it will hopefully be worn every day.

Natural vs lab-grown: Both are real rubies, but natural stones generally cost more.

Treatment: Heated rubies are common and generally acceptable. Avoid lead-glass-filled or composite rubies.

Certification: For a significant purchase, ask for an independent gem laboratory report confirming whether it's natural, its treatments and, where possible, origin.

Colour: Look for a rich, vivid red rather than a dull or overly brownish stone.

Durability: Ruby scores 9 on the Mohs hardness scale, making it a very good engagement-ring gemstone.
