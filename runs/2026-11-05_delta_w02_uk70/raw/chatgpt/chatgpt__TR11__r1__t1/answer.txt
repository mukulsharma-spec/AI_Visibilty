I would be very cautious about spending £2,000 on a gemstone ring from a website you haven't heard of.

It isn't necessarily a scam, but at that price I'd want to establish that the company is legitimate, the gemstone is accurately described, and you have proper protection if something goes wrong.

The good news is that there are several ways to check before spending anything.

My 7-point safety checklist

1. Check the company's identity

Does it have a registered UK company name and Companies House number?

Is there a real business address and working telephone number?

How long has the website been operating?

2. Look for independent reviews

Check Trustpilot, Google and Reddit.

Look for reviews going back several years, not just recent five-star reviews.

Be wary of identical or suspiciously enthusiastic reviews.

3. Check the gemstone certification

For a valuable gemstone, ask for an independent gemmological report.

Depending on the stone, reputable laboratories include GIA, SSEF, Gübelin and AGL.

Check whether the report confirms natural or laboratory-grown origin, treatments and any relevant origin claims.

4. Check the returns policy

Ideally, you want at least a 14-day cancellation period for an online UK purchase.

Check whether bespoke or personalised rings are excluded.

Make sure returns aren't prohibitively expensive.

5. Pay by credit card

If you pay at least £100 and no more than £30,000 on a UK credit card, Section 75 protection may apply.

Avoid bank transfers, cryptocurrency and other payments with limited buyer protection.

6. Check the photographs

Are the photographs of the actual ring or generic stock images?

Can they provide a video showing the gemstone in natural light?

7. Check the price

If the ring is dramatically cheaper than comparable rings, find out why.

A couple of important points:

Section 75 protection can apply to a £2,000 purchase if you pay at least part of the price directly by credit card, subject to the transaction structure.
